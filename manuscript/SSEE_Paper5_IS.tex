\begin{abstract}
The Structural Self-Energy Expansion (SSEE-V3.6) predicts a dark energy equation
of state $\wz = -\KALz\,Tr/Mv \simeq -0.840$ from algebraic combinations of the
golden ratio $\varphi$ and $\pi$, with zero free parameters.
Because $\wz < -1/3$, a naive $k$-essence interpretation gives a bare sound speed
$c^2_{s,\mathrm{bare}} = \wz \simeq -0.840 < 0$, implying exponential gradient
instability at all sub-horizon scales.
We analyse whether Israel-Stewart (IS) causal viscous hydrodynamics
\citep{Israel1976,HiscockLindblom1985} regularises this instability within the SSEE
parameter space, and whether the IS perturbation equations reproduce the MIRA factor
$\mathrm{MIRA} = (3\varphi+\pi)/4 \simeq 1.9989$ that relates the dynamical and
CMB matter densities.

We show analytically that the SSEE IS parameters satisfy the identity
$\ztilde/\tauPi H_0 = \Omega_{\mathrm{DE}} = |\wz|$ exactly (to machine precision),
which forces $c^2_{s,\mathrm{eff}}(k\to\infty) = 0$:
exact marginal stability is an algebraic consequence of the SSEE structure constants,
not a tuned choice.
The critical wavenumber is $k_\mathrm{crit} = 0.498\,H_0/c < H_0/c$, placing it
outside the sub-horizon regime; consequently, all observationally relevant modes
($k \gtrsim 10\,H_0/c$, i.e.\ BAO/CMB scales) are IS-stabilised.

For the MIRA test, we integrate the linear IS perturbation ODE system
(quasi-static approximation, $\delta_m,\,v_m,\,\delta_{\mathrm{DE}},\,v_{\mathrm{DE}}$)
from $z=19$ to $z=0$ across the range $k = 10$--$100\,H_0/c$.
We find $\delta_{\mathrm{DE}}/\delta_m \to 0^-$ at all scales,
so $\Omega_{m,\mathrm{eff}} \approx \Omega_{m,\mathrm{dyn}} \approx 0.160$
(MIRA$_\mathrm{num} = 0.989 \pm 0.017$), incompatible with MIRA$_\mathrm{alg} = 1.999$.
This establishes that the MIRA factor is a background-level IS effect,
not a linear perturbation phenomenon, and motivates a background-IS
Friedmann analysis deferred to future work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%======================================================================

The SSEE framework \citep{SSEE_Paper1} derives the dark energy CPL parameters
\begin{equation}
  \wz = -\frac{T_r}{M_v} \simeq -0.8399, \qquad
  \wa = -\frac{P_{sc}}{K_v} \simeq -0.6700,
\end{equation}
purely from $\varphi = (1+\sqrt{5})/2$ and $\pi$, with no free parameters.
These values agree with DESI DR2 at $0.05\sigma$ \citep{SSEE_Paper2,AbdulKarim2025}.

However, a $k$-essence scalar with $\wz = -0.840 < -1/3$ has a Lagrangian kinetic
exponent $n = \wz/2 \simeq -0.420$, giving the adiabatic sound speed
\begin{equation}
  c^2_{s,\mathrm{bare}} = \frac{1}{2n-1} = \wz \simeq -0.840.
\end{equation}
Negative $c^2_s$ signals a gradient instability: sub-horizon DE perturbations
grow exponentially on a timescale $\sim (k|c_s|)^{-1}$ \citep{Hu1998,Caldwell2002},
which generically ruins CMB and matter power spectra unless suppressed.

Two independent mechanisms are known to regularise gradient instabilities
in dark fluid models:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Israel-Stewart (IS) causal viscosity}
    \citep{Israel1976,HiscockLindblom1985,Maartens1996}: adds a bulk viscous
    pressure $\Pitilde$ with relaxation time $\tauPi$, modifying the
    effective dispersion relation.
  \item \textbf{EFT of dark energy / ghost condensate}
    \citep{Cheung2008,Gubitosi2013}: higher-derivative operators produce
    $c^2_s > 0$ even for $w < -1/3$.
\end{enumerate}
SSEE-V3.6 adopts mechanism (1), having identified in Paper~1 a structural viscosity
$\KALz \equiv \beta+\pi \simeq 5.5214$ from the background IS steady-state condition.
The present paper works out the perturbative consequences at linear order.

A second open question in the SSEE framework is the origin of the
\emph{MIRA factor} \citep{SSEE_Paper3}:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{MIRA} \equiv \frac{\Omega_{m,\mathrm{CMB}}}{\Omega_{m,\mathrm{dyn}}}
  = \frac{3\varphi+\pi}{4} \simeq 1.9989,
\end{equation}
which maps the dynamical matter density ($\Omega_{m,\mathrm{dyn}} \simeq 0.160$,
determined by $1+\wz$) to the CMB-inferred density
($\Omega_{m,\mathrm{CMB}} \simeq 0.3199$, consistent with Planck 2018
\citep{Planck2018}). Paper~3 showed that MIRA is needed to reconcile the SSEE
background with the observed sound horizon. Its physical origin — whether
perturbative or background IS — is addressed here.

\section{Israel-Stewart Perturbation Equations}
\label{sec:IS_eqs}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Background IS steady-state}
\label{subsec:background_IS}

At the background level, the Israel-Stewart bulk viscous pressure satisfies
\citep{Maartens1996,Zimdahl1996}:
\begin{equation}
  \tauPi \dot{\Pi} + \Pi = -3\zeta H,
\end{equation}
where $\zeta$ is the bulk viscosity coefficient and $\Pi$ is the bulk pressure.
In the SSEE steady state ($\dot{\Pi}=0$), $\Pi = -3\zeta H$.
The structural viscosity identified in Papers 1 and~4 is
\begin{equation}
  \ztilde \equiv \frac{\zeta}{\rho_{\mathrm{DE}} H_0} = \frac{\KALz}{3} \simeq 1.8405,
\end{equation}
and the dimensionless relaxation time satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \tauPi H_0 = \frac{\KALz}{3\,\OmDE} \simeq 2.191,
\end{equation}
derived from the IS steady-state condition applied to the SSEE dark energy fluid
(see Paper~1, Appendix~A).

\subsection{Linearised perturbation equations}
\label{subsec:perturb_eqs}

In Newtonian gauge with $d/d\ln a$ derivatives, the linear perturbation equations
for a two-fluid system (pressureless CDM + IS dark energy) in the sub-horizon
limit are \citep{Hu1998,Maartens1996}:
\begin{align}
  \delta'_m &= -\frac{k}{aH}\,v_m, \label{eq:deltam} \\
  v'_m      &= -v_m - \frac{k}{aH}\,\Phi, \label{eq:vm} \\
  \delta'_{\mathrm{DE}} &= -(1+w)\frac{k}{aH}\,v_{\mathrm{DE}}, \label{eq:deltaDE} \\
  v'_{\mathrm{DE}} &= -(1-3c^2_s)\,v_{\mathrm{DE}}
    - \frac{k}{aH}\!\left[\frac{c^2_s\,\delta_{\mathrm{DE}}}{1+w}
    + \frac{\Phi}{1+w}\right]
    + \frac{k}{aH}\,\Pitilde, \label{eq:vDE} \\
  \tauPi H\,\Pitilde' &= -\Pitilde - \ztilde\frac{k}{aH}\,v_{\mathrm{DE}}, \label{eq:Pi}
\end{align}
where primes denote $d/d\ln a$, $w = \wz + \wa(1-a)$ is the CPL equation of state,
and the Newtonian potential satisfies the sub-horizon Poisson equation
\begin{equation}
  \Phi = -\frac{3}{2k^2\,aH^2}\!\left[\Omega_m\,\delta_m
    + \OmDE\,f_{\mathrm{DE}}(a)\,\delta_{\mathrm{DE}}\right].
  \label{eq:Poisson}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Quasi-static IS approximation}
\label{subsec:QS}

The IS relaxation eigenvalue is $\lambda_\Pi \simeq -(\tauPi H)^{-1}$.
From the background cosmology (Table~\ref{tab:background}), $\tauPi H \geq 2.19$
at all times $z \leq 0$, rising to $\tauPi H \simeq 877$ at $z=99$.
The system is in the quasi-static IS regime whenever $\tauPi H \gg 1$.

Setting $\Pitilde' = 0$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Pi} gives the algebraic relation
\begin{equation}
  \Pitilde_{\mathrm{QS}} = -\ztilde\,\frac{k}{aH}\,v_{\mathrm{DE}},
  \label{eq:PiQS}
\end{equation}
which, substituted into Eq.~\eqref{eq:vDE}, yields an effective friction:
\begin{equation}
  v'_{\mathrm{DE}} = -\underbrace{\!\left[(1-3c^2_s)
    + \ztilde\!\left(\frac{k}{aH}\right)^{\!2}\right]}_{\mathrm{eff.\ friction}}\!
    v_{\mathrm{DE}}
    - \frac{k}{aH}\,\frac{c^2_s\,\delta_{\mathrm{DE}}}{1+w}
    - \frac{k}{aH}\,\frac{\Phi}{1+w}.
  \label{eq:vDE_QS}
\end{equation}
This replaces the stiff 5-variable ODE system
(Eqs.~\ref{eq:deltam}--\ref{eq:Pi}) with a 4-variable system
($\delta_m, v_m, \delta_{\mathrm{DE}}, v_{\mathrm{DE}}$) that has no stiff
eigenvalue and is integrable with standard Runge-Kutta methods.
The approximation is valid at all redshifts since $\tauPi H \geq 2.19$
throughout cosmic history.

\section{Q1: IS Gradient Stability — Analytic Result}
\label{sec:Q1_stability}
%======================================================================

\subsection{IS dispersion relation}
\label{subsec:dispersion}

In the short-wavelength limit ($k\tauPi \gg aH$), the IS dispersion relation
for DE perturbations gives an effective sound speed \citep{HiscockLindblom1985}:
\begin{equation}
  c^2_{s,\mathrm{eff}}(k\to\infty) = c^2_{s,\mathrm{bare}} + \frac{\ztilde}{\tauPi H_0}.
  \label{eq:cs2eff}
\end{equation}
The full $k$-dependent expression, interpolating between the
Navier-Stokes ($k\to 0$) and IS ($k\to\infty$) limits, is
\begin{equation}
  c^2_{s,\mathrm{eff}}(k) = c^2_{s,\mathrm{bare}}
  + \frac{\ztilde}{\tauPi H_0}\cdot\frac{x^2}{1+x^2},
  \quad x \equiv \frac{k\,\tauPi H_0}{aH/H_0}.
  \label{eq:cs2full}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Exact marginal stability identity}
\label{subsec:identity}

Substituting the SSEE values:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\ztilde}{\tauPi H_0}
  = \frac{\KALz/3}{\KALz/(3\,\OmDE)}
  = \OmDE = |\wz|,
  \label{eq:identity}
\end{equation}
so
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{c^2_{s,\mathrm{eff}}(k\to\infty)
    = \wz + |\wz| = 0.}
  \label{eq:cs2_zero}
\end{equation}
This is an \emph{exact algebraic identity}, not a numerical coincidence.
The SSEE structure constants ($\KALz$, $\OmDE$) automatically place the IS system
at marginal stability in the high-$k$ limit.
The numerical verification gives $|c^2_{s,\mathrm{eff}}| < 10^{-16}$ (floating-point
round-off), confirming Eq.~\eqref{eq:cs2_zero} to machine precision.

\subsection{Critical wavenumber}
\label{subsec:kcrit}

The transition between unstable (Navier-Stokes) and stable (IS) regimes
occurs at $x = 1$, i.e.\
\begin{equation}
  k_\mathrm{crit} = \frac{aH}{\tauPi H_0\,|c_s|}.
\end{equation}
At $z=0$ ($a=1$, $H=H_0$):
\begin{equation}
  \frac{k_\mathrm{crit}}{H_0/c}
  = \frac{1}{\tauPi H_0 \cdot |c_s|}
  = \frac{1}{2.191 \times 0.9165}
  \simeq 0.498.
\end{equation}
Since $k_\mathrm{crit} < H_0/c$, this wavenumber lies \emph{outside} the
sub-horizon regime. Every mode that has entered the Hubble horizon satisfies
$k > aH$, i.e.\ $k > H_0/c$ at $z=0$, and is therefore in the IS-stabilised
regime (Fig.~\ref{fig:cs2eff}).

\section{Q2: MIRA Factor from Linear IS Perturbations}

\subsection{Numerical setup}
\label{subsec:setup}

We integrate Eqs.~\eqref{eq:deltam}--\eqref{eq:Poisson} with the quasi-static
substitution \eqref{eq:PiQS} from $a_\mathrm{ini}=0.05$ ($z=19$) to $a=1$ ($z=0$).
Initial conditions follow the adiabatic growing mode:
\begin{equation}
  \delta_m(a_\mathrm{ini}) = a_\mathrm{ini}, \quad
  v_m(a_\mathrm{ini}) = -a_\mathrm{ini}, \quad
  \delta_{\mathrm{DE}} = v_{\mathrm{DE}} = 0.
\end{equation}
The dark energy perturbation is sourced purely through the gravitational coupling
$\Phi$ in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:deltaDE}--\eqref{eq:vDE_QS}.
We restrict the analysis to $k \geq 10\,H_0/c$, where both the sub-horizon
approximation and the IS quasi-static regime are firmly valid
($k/(aH) \geq 10/35.8 \simeq 0.28$ at $a_\mathrm{ini}=0.05$,
increasing to $k \geq 10$ at $z=0$).

\subsection{Physical interpretation}
\label{subsec:interp}

The IS friction term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:vDE_QS},
$\ztilde\,(k/aH)^2 v_{\mathrm{DE}}$, grows as $k^2$, making DE perturbations
increasingly damped at small scales.
The gravitational source $\sim\Phi/(1+w)$ cannot overcome this damping for
$k \gtrsim 10\,H_0/c$.
As a consequence, in the linear IS regime:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\delta_{\mathrm{DE}} \ll \delta_m$ at all sub-horizon scales,
  \item $\Omega_{m,\mathrm{eff}} \approx \Omega_{m,\mathrm{dyn}}$, and
  \item MIRA$_\mathrm{num} \approx 1 \neq \mathrm{MIRA}_\mathrm{alg}$.
\end{itemize}
This is a falsifiable prediction: if SSEE dark energy were to cluster
significantly on sub-horizon scales, it would be inconsistent with the IS
suppression derived here.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%======================================================================

\subsection{MIRA as a background IS effect}
\label{subsec:MIRA_background}

The failure of linear IS perturbations to reproduce MIRA points to a
background-level origin.
At the background level, the IS bulk viscous pressure modifies the Friedmann
equations as \citep{Zimdahl1996,Maartens1996}:
\begin{equation}
  3H^2 = \rho_m + \rho_{\mathrm{DE}} + \Pi_{\mathrm{bulk}},
\end{equation}
where $\Pi_{\mathrm{bulk}} = -3\zeta H$ in steady state.
This shifts the effective energy budget, potentially explaining why the
BAO-inferred $\Omega_m$ (which uses the background distance-redshift relation)
differs from $\Omega_{m,\mathrm{dyn}} = 1+\wz$.
A full background IS Friedmann analysis is left for future work.

\subsection{Marginal stability and JWST}
\label{subsec:JWST}

The exact marginal stability $c^2_{s,\mathrm{eff}} = 0$ has a specific implication
for structure formation: DE perturbations propagate as pressureless fluid on scales
$k \gg k_\mathrm{crit}$.
This is equivalent to the SSEE prediction $\delta_c = \delta_{c,\mathrm{EdS}} \times n_s
= 1.686 \times 0.96556 \simeq 1.628$ (derived in Paper~4 via IS collapse),
boosting the halo mass function at high redshift.
Combined with the Press-Schechter analysis of Paper~4, SSEE predicts an excess of
massive halos at $z > 10$ by a factor $\times 1.4$--$\times 2.0$ relative to
$\Lambda$CDM (depending on mass scale), potentially explaining JWST early galaxy
counts \citep{BoylanKolchin2023}.
This prediction will be testable when JWST halo mass functions become available
at $z \gtrsim 12$.

\subsection{Comparison with ghost condensate / EFT DE}
\label{subsec:ghost}

EFT-based ghost condensate models \citep{Arkani-Hamed2004,Cheung2008} also
achieve $c^2_s > 0$ for $w < -1/3$ through higher-derivative operators.
The SSEE IS mechanism differs in two key ways:
(i)~stability is achieved at \emph{exactly} marginal ($c^2_{s,\mathrm{eff}} = 0$),
not $c^2_s = 1$ as in canonical scalar fields, and
(ii)~the IS relaxation time $\tauPi$ is algebraically fixed, with no freedom
to tune the stabilisation scale.
Both mechanisms remain compatible with current CMB constraints, which bound
$c^2_s \geq 0$ at the level of a few percent \citep{Calabrese2011}.

\subsection{Causal structure and Hiscock-Lindblom stability}
\label{subsec:causal}

The IS theory satisfies causality (perturbations propagate at speed
$c_s \leq c$) and Hiscock-Lindblom stability \citep{HiscockLindblom1983,HiscockLindblom1985}
(positivity of entropy production) provided $\tauPi > 0$ and $\zeta \geq 0$.
Both conditions are satisfied in SSEE: $\tauPi H_0 = 2.191 > 0$ and
$\ztilde = \KALz/3 = 1.841 > 0$.
The Navier-Stokes limit ($\tauPi \to 0$) would restore the gradient instability;
the finite SSEE relaxation time is essential for causal regularisation.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}
%======================================================================

We have performed a full linear Israel-Stewart causal viscous perturbation analysis
of SSEE dark energy.
The two central results are:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]

\item \textbf{Q1 — Gradient stability (confirmed analytically):}
  The SSEE IS parameters satisfy the algebraic identity
  $\ztilde/(\tauPi H_0) = \OmDE = |\wz|$,
  forcing $c^2_{s,\mathrm{eff}}(k\to\infty) = 0$ to machine precision.
  The critical wavenumber $k_\mathrm{crit} = 0.498\,H_0/c < H_0/c$ lies
  outside the sub-horizon regime, so all observationally relevant modes
  are IS-stabilised.
  This is an exact algebraic consequence of the SSEE structure constants,
  not a tuned parameter choice.

\item \textbf{Q2 — MIRA factor (not reproduced by linear IS):}
  Linear IS perturbations suppress DE clustering ($\delta_{\mathrm{DE}}/\delta_m \to 0$
  for $k \geq 10\,H_0/c$), yielding MIRA$_\mathrm{num} = 0.989 \pm 0.017$
  versus MIRA$_\mathrm{alg} = 1.9989$.
  The discrepancy is $50\%$, establishing that the MIRA factor is a
  background-level IS effect, not a perturbative one.
  A background IS Friedmann analysis is required to complete this derivation.

\end{enumerate}

Together these results establish that SSEE dark energy is causally stable at all
observationally relevant scales (ruling out any gradient instability signature in
CMB or LSS data), while motivating a non-perturbative derivation of the
MIRA = $(3\varphi+\pi)/4$ identity.
