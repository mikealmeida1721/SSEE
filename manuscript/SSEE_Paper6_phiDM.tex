\begin{abstract}
The Structural Self-Energy Expansion (\SSEE) framework derives all cosmological
parameters algebraically from the golden ratio $\varphi$ and $\pi$, achieving a
zero-free-parameter dark energy background (Papers~1--5) that has demonstrated
strong coincidence with CMB, BAO, and cluster tests.
Paper~5, using the CMB matter density $\Omega_{\rm m,CMB}=0.309$ in the Poisson
growth source, yields $\sigma_8 = 0.834$, $S_8 = 0.846$ ($3.5\sigma$ KiDS-1000 and
DES-Y3), and a mean $f\sigma_8$ tension of $0.70\sigma$ across six RSD surveys ---
essentially identical to $\Lambda$CDM in the growth-rate sector.  The open observational
challenge is therefore the \emph{lensing amplitude}: $S_8 = 0.846$ overshoots
KiDS-1000 by $3.5\sigma$.
We systematically scan EFT extensions (kinetic braiding $\alpha_B$, run-rate $\alpha_M$)
and find that kinetic braiding is a no-go: it suppresses $f\sigma_8$ without reducing the
lensing amplitude.
We then propose a \emph{two-sector} dark matter model in which $\Omega_{\rm CDM} = 0.160$
is supplemented by a $\phi$-dark matter component
$\Omega_{\phiDM} = \Omega_{\rm m,CMB} - \Omega_{\rm m,dyn} = 0.149$, active only on scales $k < \kfs$.
The CMB matter density itself is a forward identity, $\Omega_{\rm m,CMB}=\omega_m/h^2$
with $\omega_m=\omega_b+\omega_c+\omega_\nu$ and $\omega_c=\mathrm{KAL}_0\,\omega_b\,n_s$
(Paper~1; $0.41\sigma$ from Planck), so the two-sector split is a \emph{difference}
of two independent predictions, not a factor.
The $\phi$-DM mass is a \emph{forward prediction} written into a free scalar
Lagrangian: $\mphi = \mnu^{\rm active}\times(\mathrm{SOLAR}^2\cdot\mathrm{KRYSTOS})
= 0.0685\,\mathrm{eV}\times 594.28 = 40.70\,\mathrm{eV}$, where the multiplier is a
\emph{pure} $(\varphi,\pi)$ number and $\mnu^{\rm active}=\mathcal{R}_2\times0.9530\,\mathrm{eV}$
with $\mathcal{R}_2=\Omega_{\rm DNAV}/(\mathrm{KAL}\cdot\mathrm{TRIAL})$. Nothing is fitted
to data; the particle's own temperature $T_\phi = 0.539\,T_\nu$ follows from the
relic-abundance constraint. A direct two-sector CLASS Boltzmann run with this
particle (zero fitted parameters) gives $\sigma_8 = 0.747$ and
$S_8 = 0.758$ --- in $\mathbf{0.04\sigma}$ agreement with KiDS-1000 ($0.759\pm0.024$),
\emph{resolving} the $S_8$ lensing tension from the $3.5\sigma$ single-sector baseline.
The free-streaming suppression scale is a CLASS \emph{output}, not an input: a Viel
fit to the particle/cold $P(k)$ ratio returns $\alpha = 1.117\,$Mpc$/h$, corresponding
to $\kfs = 0.754\,h/$Mpc --- the primary falsifiable prediction for DESI~Y3 / Euclid
(2026--2028). Because $m_\phi\simeq 41$~eV makes the particle non-relativistic at
$z_{\rm nr}\sim10^5 \gg z_{\rm rec}$, it behaves as cold dark matter for the CMB: the
acoustic peaks shift by only $\sim1\,\ell$ relative to
$\Lambda$CDM. The same free-streaming that resolves $S_8$ also reaches the
$\sigma_8(R{=}8)$ window probed by RSD, so the two-sector $f\sigma_8$ tension rises
modestly to $0.93\sigma$ (from the $0.70\sigma$ single-sector baseline), still
$<1\sigma$ and close to $\Lambda$CDM ($0.73\sigma$): the deliberate cost of lowering
$\sigma_8$ to fix the lensing amplitude. We explicitly note that the reported TT residual is
computed against our own CLASS $\Lambda$CDM rather than a full Planck \texttt{plik}
likelihood with covariance; a paper-grade CMB validation is deferred.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The Structural Self-Energy Expansion (\SSEE) framework \citep{SSEE_Paper1} is a
zero-free-parameter dark energy model in which the equation-of-state parameters
$(\wz, \wa) = (-0.840, -0.670)$ are derived algebraically from $\varphi$ (golden
ratio) and $\pi$. The framework has passed four independent observational tests:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Paper~2} \citep{SSEE_Paper2}: DESI DR2 \citep{AbdulKarim2025} BAO ($0.24\sigma$ Pantheon+) and
    galaxy cluster mass discrepancies ($\chi^2_r = 0.122$).
  \item \textbf{Paper~3} \citep{SSEE_Paper3}: CMB Planck PR4 (plik\_lite
    $\Delta\mathrm{BIC} = -32.9$ $\omega_m$-direct (full \texttt{plik} MCMC; $-23.9$ \texttt{plik\_lite}), in favour of \SSEE\ vs.\ $\Lambda$CDM).
  \item \textbf{Paper~4} \citep{SSEE_Paper4}: Algebraic derivation of $n_s$, $H_0$,
    $\Omega_m$, $\Omega_b h^2$ from the Nine Sovereignties.
  \item \textbf{Paper~5} \citep{SSEE_Paper5}: Israel-Stewart \citep{IsraelStewart1979} causal gradient stability
    ($c^2_{s,\rm eff} = 0$ exact), growth factor $\mathcal{G}=1.0032$ (slight enhancement vs.\ $\Lambda$CDM),
    $\sigma_8 = 0.834$, $S_8 = 0.846$ ($3.5\sigma$ KiDS; single-sector open lensing tension).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The residual tension}
\label{subsec:tension}

Paper~5 corrected the growth equation to use the CMB matter density
$\Omega_{\rm m,CMB} = \omega_m/h^2 = 0.309$ in the Poisson source,
yielding $\mathcal{G} = D_1^{\rm SSEE}/D_1^{\Lambda{\rm CDM}} = 1.0032$ (slight enhancement),
$\sigma_8 = 0.834$, and a mean $f\sigma_8$ tension of $0.70\sigma$ across six RSD surveys
\citep{Beutler2012,Howlett2015,Alam2017,Hou2021} --- essentially the same as
$\Lambda$CDM ($0.73\sigma$).  The growth-rate sector is therefore already
consistent at the single-sector level; the two-sector free-streaming introduced
below lowers $\sigma_8$ to resolve $S_8$ and, since that suppression reaches RSD
scales, raises the $f\sigma_8$ tension modestly to $0.93\sigma$ (still $<1\sigma$).

The remaining observational challenge is the \emph{lensing amplitude}: the
single-sector $S_8 = 0.834 \times \sqrt{0.309/0.3} = 0.846$ lies $3.5\sigma$ above
KiDS-1000 ($0.759 \pm 0.024$) \citep{KiDS2021} and above DES-Y3
($0.776 \pm 0.017$) \citep{DESY32022}.  This is the $S_8$--lensing tension.

This paper presents a two-sector model that \emph{resolves} the $S_8$ lensing
tension within the minimal-parameter philosophy of \SSEE\ (no growth-data fit; the
$\phi$-DM mass is a forward prediction written into a free scalar Lagrangian, and
the residual ans\"atze are explicit and tracked as OP-9/11/14 in
\texttt{OPEN\_PROBLEMS.md}).  A direct two-sector CLASS Boltzmann run with the
particle's own relic temperature yields $S_8 = 0.758$, in $0.04\sigma$ agreement
with KiDS-1000, via a free-streaming suppression that emerges as a CLASS output at
$k > \kfs = 0.754\,h/$Mpc.

\subsection{Strategy}
\label{subsec:strategy}

We follow three steps, each falsifying hypotheses in sequence:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{EFT extensions}: kinetic braiding $\alpha_B$ and run-rate $\alpha_M$
    in the Bellini-Sawicki \citep{Bellini2015} parameterisation.
    We show that kinetic braiding suppresses the growth factor and worsens the
    $S_8$ lensing tension; $\alpha_M$ achieves marginal improvement only near
    the boundary of current observational bounds.  EFT extensions are therefore
    ruled out as the primary mechanism.
  \item \textbf{$\phi$-dark matter hypothesis}: the CMB matter density is the
    forward identity $\Omega_{\rm m,CMB} = \omega_m/h^2 = 0.309$
    (with $\omega_c = \mathrm{KAL}_0\,\omega_b\,n_s$, Paper~1), independent of the
    dynamical value $\Omega_{\rm m,dyn} = 0.160$. Their difference defines a second
    dark-matter component, $\Omega_{\phiDM} = \Omega_{\rm m,CMB} - \Omega_{\rm m,dyn}
    = 0.149$, active only below a free-streaming wavenumber $\kfs$.
  \item \textbf{Mass forward prediction}: combining the active neutrino mass
    $\mnu^{\rm active} = \mathcal{R}_2 \times 0.9530\,\mathrm{eV} = 0.0685\,\mathrm{eV}$
    (with $\mathcal{R}_2 = \Omega_{\rm DNAV}/(\mathrm{KAL}\cdot\mathcal{T}) = 0.07188$,
    a pure $(\varphi,\pi)$ ratio) with the pure dimensionless multiplier
    $\mathrm{SOLAR}^2\cdot\mathrm{KRYSTOS} = 594.28$, we obtain the
    forward prediction
    $\mphi = \mnu^{\rm active}\,(\mathrm{SOLAR}^2\cdot\mathrm{KRYSTOS})
    = 40.70\,\mathrm{eV}$.  Because the multiplier is a \emph{pure number}, the
    product carries the units of $\mnu^{\rm active}$ (eV) without dimensional
    repair, and the coefficient is exactly the mass term of a free scalar
    Lagrangian (Sec.~\ref{subsec:lagrangian}).  Nothing is fitted to growth data; the
    residual question of why this particular multiplier (the Type~P status of
    $\Sigma m_\nu$) is tracked as OP-14 in \texttt{OPEN\_PROBLEMS.md}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{SSEE-V3.6: Algebraic Background}
\label{sec:background}

We collect the \SSEE\ algebraic quantities relevant to this paper. Define the
structural constants:
\begin{align}
  \varphi &= \tfrac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2} \approx 1.6180, &
  \pi &\approx 3.1416, \notag\\
  \beta &= \tfrac{\varphi+\pi}{2} \approx 2.3798, &
  \mathcal{K} &= \varphi + \pi + (\pi+\varphi) \approx 9.5192, \notag\\
  \mathcal{T} &= 3(\varphi+\beta) \approx 11.9935, &
  \mathcal{M}_V &= \varphi + \pi + \mathcal{K} \approx 14.2789.
\end{align}
These yield the equation of state:
\begin{equation}
  w_0 = -\frac{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{M}_V} = -0.8400, \qquad
  w_a = -\frac{\pi + 2\varphi}{\mathcal{K}} = -0.6699,
\end{equation}
and the dynamical matter density $\Omega_{\rm m,dyn} = 1 + w_0 = 0.1600$.

\subsection{Relevant Paper 4 quantities}
\label{subsec:paper4}

Paper~4 derived the following from the stability ratio
$\mathcal{R}_2 = \Omega_{\rm DNAV}/(\mathrm{KAL}_0\cdot\mathcal{T}) = 0.07188$
(a pure $(\varphi,\pi)$ number, with $\Omega_{\rm DNAV} = \pi+\varphi \approx 4.7596$,

\begin{align}
  \Omega_b h^2_{\rm SSEE} &= \frac{\pi-\varphi}{3(\varphi+\pi)^2}
    = \frac{\pi-\varphi}{H_0^{\rm SSEE}} = 0.02242, \\
  \tau_\Pi H_0 &= \frac{\mathrm{KAL}_0}{3\,\Omega_{\rm DE}} = 2.191 \quad
    [\text{IS relaxation time, pure number}], \\
  \mnu^{\rm active} &= \mathcal{R}_2\,\Omega_b h^2\,\frac{93.14\,\mathrm{eV}}{\tau_\Pi H_0}
    = \mathcal{R}_2 \times 0.9530\,\mathrm{eV} = 0.0685\,\mathrm{eV}.
\end{align}
Here $\Omega_b h^2 \cdot 93.14/(\tau_\Pi H_0) = 0.9530\,\mathrm{eV}$ is a fixed
mass scale (the factor $93.14\,\mathrm{eV}$ converting relic density to mass; the
ratio $\tau_\Pi H_0$ is dimensionless so $H_0$ cancels its time units).

\subsection{Two independent matter densities and the two-sector deficit}
\label{subsec:mira}

\SSEE\ predicts \emph{two} matter densities by independent routes, with no factor
relating them (Paper~1, Sec.~1.4, \textit{Two-$\Omega_m$ Criterion}). The
dynamical-sector value follows from the equation of state,
\begin{equation}
  \Omega_{\rm m,dyn} = 1 + w_0 = 0.160 \qquad [\text{DESI / background}],
\end{equation}
while the CMB matter density is the forward identity
\begin{equation}
  \Omega_{\rm m,CMB} = \frac{\omega_m}{h^2}, \qquad
  \omega_m = \omega_b + \omega_c + \omega_\nu, \qquad
  \omega_c = \mathrm{KAL}_0\,\omega_b\,n_s,
  \label{eq:omega_m_cmb}
\end{equation}
with $\omega_b = (\pi-\varphi)/[3(\varphi+\pi)^2] = 0.02242$ (Paper~1),
$n_s = 1-\varphi^{-7} = 0.9656$, and $\omega_\nu = \mnu^{\rm active}/93.14 = 0.00074$,
giving $\omega_m = 0.1427$ and $\Omega_{\rm m,CMB} = 0.1427/0.6796^2 = 0.309$ at
$\Halg = 67.962$. This matches Planck ($\Omega_{m,\rm CMB} = 0.315$
\citep{Planck2018}; $\omega_c$ is $0.41\sigma$ from the Planck value) without any
matter factor: the earlier mapping $\Omega_{\rm m,CMB} = \MIRA\times\Omega_{\rm m,dyn}$
is retired (OP-8 dissolved), and the MIRA scalar
$\MIRA = (3\varphi+\pi)/4 = 1.9989$ now enters only the Hubble screening
$f_{\rm screen}$ of Paper~9, consistent with its ``mirror'' role there.

\textbf{Physical mechanism.}
Because $\Omega_{\rm m,CMB} = 0.309$ and $\Omega_{\rm m,dyn} = 0.160$ are two
\emph{independent} predictions, their difference,
\begin{equation}
  \Omega_{\phiDM} = \Omega_{\rm m,CMB} - \Omega_{\rm m,dyn}
    = 0.309 - 0.160 = 0.149,
  \label{eq:two_sector}
\end{equation}
is the abundance that must be carried by an additional dark-sector component which
couples to the CMB metric perturbations but undergoes free-streaming suppression in
the growth sector \citep{Hu1998}. The $\varphi$-DM field
(Sec.~\ref{subsec:two_sector}) with forward-predicted mass
$\mphi = 40.70\,\mathrm{eV}$ provides exactly this component. The two sectors are
\emph{not} equal ($\Omega_{\rm CDM} = 0.160$, $\Omega_{\phiDM} = 0.149$); the split
is a difference of two forward predictions, not an imposed factor of two.

\textbf{Open derivation step.}
The relic abundance $\Omega_{\phiDM}h^2 = 0.069$ computed from $\mphi$ and the SSEE
reheating temperature (without assuming a fixed sector ratio) is deferred to
Paper~B (quintessential inflation and reheating). The \emph{testability} of the
two-sector deficit is the prediction $\kfs = 0.754\,h/\mathrm{Mpc}$
(falsifiable by DESI~Y3/Euclid, 2026--2028).

\section{EFT Extension Scan}
\label{sec:eft}

Before proposing the two-sector model we systematically rule out simpler EFT
extensions within the Bellini-Sawicki \citep{Bellini2015,Gubitosi2013} parameterisation,
which characterises modifications of gravity by four functions
$(\alpha_K, \alpha_B, \alpha_M, \alpha_T)$.

\subsection{Kinetic braiding ($\alpha_B$)}
\label{subsec:braiding}

Kinetic braiding modifies the effective Newton constant as
$G_{\rm eff}/G_N = 1 + \alpha_B\,\Omega_{\rm DE}/\Omega_m$. This enhances
clustering and was identified in Paper~5 as a candidate for boosting $f\sigma_8$.

We compute the algebraic value of $\alpha_B$ from the ratio of the CDM density
to the dark energy density in \SSEE:
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_B^{\rm SSEE} = \frac{\Omega_{\rm m,dyn}}{\Omega_{\rm DE}} - 1
    = \frac{0.160}{0.840} - 1 \approx -0.810,
\end{equation}
which is \emph{negative}. A negative $\alpha_B$ suppresses $G_{\rm eff}$, worsening
the tension. The 2D scan of $(\alpha_B, \alpha_M)$ space confirms that no
configuration with purely kinetic braiding reduces the mean $f\sigma_8$ tension
below $3.29\sigma$ while satisfying the no-ghost condition
$\alpha_K + \tfrac{3}{2}\alpha_B^2 \geq 0$.

\begin{tcolorbox}[result, title={No-go: Kinetic braiding}]
  Kinetic braiding at the algebraic \SSEE\ value $\alpha_B = -0.810$ is a
  \textbf{no-go}: it suppresses $G_{\rm eff}$, increasing the $f\sigma_8$
  tension above the $0.70\sigma$ baseline (Paper~5, corrected; scan minimum $\sim3.3\sigma$, far from $0.70\sigma$).
\end{tcolorbox}

\subsection{Run-rate modification ($\alpha_M$)}
\label{subsec:alphaM}

The run-rate $\alpha_M \equiv d\ln M_\star^2/d\ln a$ modifies the Planck mass
running and enters the growth equation through an effective friction term. A 2D
scan over $(\alpha_B \in [-1, 0],\, \alpha_M \in [-0.15, 0.15])$ shows a minimum
tension of $1.57\sigma$ at $(\alpha_B = 0,\, \alpha_M = -0.08)$.

However, $|\alpha_M| = 0.08$ is at the boundary of current Planck/GW constraints
($|\alpha_M| \lesssim 0.08$), and the value lacks an algebraic derivation from
$\varphi$ and $\pi$. We therefore do not adopt this route.

\section{The $\phi$-Dark Matter Hypothesis}
\label{sec:phiDM}

We propose that the matter deficit $\Omega_{\rm m,CMB}-\Omega_{\rm m,dyn}$ is a second
dark matter component —
\emph{$\phi$-dark matter} ($\phiDM$) — coupled to the IS relaxation sector and
decoupling at redshift $z_{\rm dec}$ to produce a warm dark matter component
\citep{Bode2001} with free-streaming scale $\kfs$.

\subsection{Two-sector structure}
\label{subsec:two_sector}

The two-sector model is defined by:
\begin{align}
  \Omega_{\rm CDM}    &= \Omega_{\rm m,dyn} = 0.160 \quad [\text{cold, always active}],\\
  \Omega_{\phiDM}     &= \Omega_{\rm m,CMB} - \Omega_{\rm m,dyn} = 0.149
    \quad [\text{warm, active for } k < \kfs],\\
  \Omega_{\rm m,total} &= \Omega_{\rm m,CMB} = 0.309 \quad [\text{CMB and BAO scales}].
\end{align}
The key observation is that the two sectors contribute \emph{differently} at
different scales:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RSD scales} ($k \sim 0.01$--$0.1\,h/\mathrm{Mpc} \ll \kfs$):
    both sectors are active and cluster. The effective matter density for growth
    is $\Omega_{\rm m,growth} = 0.309$, restoring $f(z)$ to
    $\Lambda$CDM-compatible values.
  \item \textbf{$\sigma_8$ scale} ($k = 0.125\,h/\mathrm{Mpc} < \kfs$):
    at this scale, the cold sector clusters fully while the warm $\phi$-DM sector
    is partially free-streamed. Rather than rely on an analytic growth factor, we
    read $\sigma_8$ \emph{directly} from a two-sector \textsc{class} run with the
    particle's own relic temperature $T_\phi$ (Section~\ref{subsec:mass_derivation}),
    obtaining $\sigeff = 0.747$ and
    $S_8 = \sigeff\sqrt{\Omega_{\rm m}/0.3} = 0.758$ --- within $0.04\sigma$ of the
    KiDS value $0.759\pm0.024$ (Section~\ref{subsec:falsifiable}).
\end{itemize}

generated-mass term $g^2 v$ --- built entirely from $\varphi$
and $\pi$. The active neutrino scale is

distinction from the earlier $\mphi=\mnu\times\Halg$ pattern, in which the factor
$\Halg=3(\varphi+\pi)^2$ carried Hubble units $[\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}]$

is a number from the start, and the mass is a genuine \emph{forward prediction}
with zero quantities fit to data.

\subsection{Explicit Lagrangian and field content}
\label{subsec:lagrangian}

The mass relation~\eqref{eq:mass_algebraic} fixes a number; it does not, by
itself, specify the field.  We now make the field content explicit.
We model $\phi$-dark matter as a single real cosmological scalar field $\chi$
(distinct from the SSEE background $k$-essence scalar $\phi$ of Paper~7; both
are cosmological scalars and neither is a WIMP, QCD-axion, or SUSY relic),
minimally coupled to gravity, with the action
\begin{equation}
  S_{\phiDM} = \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,
    \left[
      -\tfrac{1}{2}\,g^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\mu\chi\,\partial_\nu\chi
      -\tfrac{1}{2}\,\mphi^2\,\chi^2
      -\tfrac{1}{2}\,\xi\,R\,\chi^2
    \right],
  \label{eq:phiDM_lagrangian}
\end{equation}
where $R$ is the Ricci scalar and $\xi$ is a non-minimal coupling
($\xi = 0$ for pure minimal coupling; $\xi = 1/6$ for conformal coupling).
The mass term $\tfrac{1}{2}\mphi^2\chi^2$ is the standard quadratic term of a
spin-0 boson, with the coefficient fixed by the forward prediction of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mass_algebraic}:
\begin{equation}
  \mphi = \mnu^{\rm active}\,
          \bigl(\mathrm{SOLAR}^2\cdot\mathrm{KRYSTOS}\bigr) = 40.70\,\mathrm{eV}.
  \label{eq:mass_coeff}
\end{equation}
Because the multiplier is a pure $(\varphi,\pi)$ number, this coefficient is
written \emph{into} the action rather than appended to it: the mass parameter of
the free scalar $\chi$ \emph{is} the algebraic prediction.  No renormalisable
portal coupling to the Standard Model is included: $\chi$ interacts only
gravitationally.

\paragraph{Generated-mass structure.}
The factorisation $\mathrm{SOLAR}^2\cdot\mathrm{KRYSTOS}$ is not arbitrary: it has
the form of a \emph{generated} mass term $\mphi \sim g^2\,v$, with the squared
coupling $g^2 = \mathrm{SOLAR}^2$ (the radiative-pulse entity $\mathrm{SOLAR}=
\varphi+2\pi$ appearing squared, as a coupling does) acting on the scale
$v = \mathrm{KRYSTOS}\,\mnu^{\rm active}$ set by the constraint entity
$\mathrm{KRYSTOS}=2\,\Omega_{\rm DNAV}$ and the active-neutrino unit. The
enhancement (not a loop suppression $g^2/16\pi^2$) lifts $\chi$ from the neutrino
scale to $\sim 41$~eV. The mass is therefore the coefficient of an explicit
free-scalar action with the structure of a generated mass --- which is what
\emph{advances} OP-9 from a bare numerical coincidence to a written term. What
remains genuinely open (the refined OP-9) is deriving the coefficient
$\mathrm{SOLAR}^2\cdot\mathrm{KRYSTOS}$ from the dissipative ($\mathrm{KAL}$-governed)
transport sector rather than reading it from the closed dictionary.
Two features of Eq.~\eqref{eq:phiDM_lagrangian} are essential below.
First, $\chi$ is a \emph{boson}, not a fermion --- which excludes the
active--sterile mixing production channel.
Second, the action contains a single free continuous parameter, the
non-minimal coupling $\xi$ (tracked as OP-11 in
\texttt{OPEN\_PROBLEMS.md}); its role is constrained below by the production
mechanism.

\subsection{Production mechanism: the Dodelson--Widrow route is excluded}
\label{subsec:production}

A relic of mass $\mphi = 40.70\,\mathrm{eV}$ contributing
$\Omega_{\phiDM}h^2 \simeq 0.069$ can in principle be populated by two
mechanisms.  We tested both against the requirement that no additional
\emph{continuous} free parameter beyond those already admitted as open problems
($m_\phi$ itself, OP-9; $\xi$, OP-11) be introduced.

\paragraph{(a) Dodelson--Widrow (non-resonant mixing) --- tested, excluded.}
The Dodelson--Widrow mechanism \citep{DodelsonWidrow1994} produces the relic
through active--sterile oscillations, which requires $\chi$ to be a fermion
and introduces a mixing angle $\theta$.  Matching the observed abundance with
the Boyarsky--Ruchayskiy--Shaposhnikov relation \citep{Boyarsky2009} fixes
\begin{equation}
  \sin^2(2\theta)_{\rm DW} \;\simeq\; 4.78\times10^{-5}.
  \label{eq:sin2theta_DW}
\end{equation}
For the DW route to be consistent with the rest of the SSEE programme,
$\sin^2(2\theta)_{\rm DW}$ would have to admit a closed algebraic form in
$\varphi,\pi$ (otherwise it would constitute a \emph{new} continuous fitted
parameter, in addition to the ones already admitted as open problems).
A scan of $\sim 80$ monomial combinations of $\varphi,\pi,\Omega,\MIRA$ (script
\texttt{ssee\_paperB\_DW.py}) returns a best candidate $\varphi^{-21} =
4.09\times10^{-5}$, which differs from Eq.~\eqref{eq:sin2theta_DW} by
$14.6\%$ --- beyond the $10\%$ threshold adopted for a ``clean'' SSEE
identity.  \textbf{This is a negative result}: the DW mixing angle does
\emph{not} reduce to the SSEE algebra.  Were $\phi$-DM a DW sterile neutrino,
$\sin^2(2\theta)$ would be an irreducible free parameter, inconsistent with
the minimal-parameter philosophy that already tolerates only the ans\"atze
catalogued in \texttt{OPEN\_PROBLEMS.md}.  The DW route is therefore excluded
on internal-consistency grounds, independently of any observational bound.

\paragraph{(b) Gravitational particle production --- adopted.}
The remaining channel consistent with the action~\eqref{eq:phiDM_lagrangian}
is gravitational particle production \citep{Parker1968,ChungKolbRiotto1998}:
the time-dependent metric during the inflation-to-reheating transition
populates the $\chi$ field directly, with \emph{no} mixing angle.  The relic
abundance depends only on $\mphi$ and the inflationary potential
$V(\phi)$ --- and in SSEE both are already fixed
($\mphi$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:mass_algebraic}; the $\alpha$-attractor potential
with $\alpha = \varphi^4/3$ by Paper~1).  This route introduces no free
parameter and selects $\chi$ as a scalar, consistently with
Eq.~\eqref{eq:phiDM_lagrangian}.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Honest scope statement.}  We have established \emph{which} mechanism
is compatible with zero parameters (gravitational production) and \emph{which}
is excluded (Dodelson--Widrow).  We have \emph{not} computed the full
gravitational-production integral that would yield $\Omega_{\phiDM}h^2$ from
$\mphi$ and $V(\phi)$ \emph{ab initio}; an order-of-magnitude estimate is
consistent with the required abundance, but the complete calculation
(including the $\xi$-dependence) is deferred to a dedicated study (Paper~B).
The free-streaming scale $\kfs$ used in Section~\ref{subsec:lya} is therefore
retained as an order-of-magnitude estimate, as already caveated there.

\section{Observational Verification}
\label{sec:verification}

We solve the linear growth equation for two limits and construct $\fsig(z)$
for each.

\subsection{Growth equations}
\label{subsec:growth}

The growth-factor ODE in the conformal Newton gauge is
$\ddot{D} + \mathcal{H}\dot{D} - \tfrac{3}{2}\mathcal{H}^2\Omega_m(a)\,D = 0$,
where dots denote derivatives with respect to $\ln a$.

For all observationally relevant scales ($k < \kfs$), both dark matter sectors
contribute to the growth of structure. The relevant regime is:

\begin{description}
  \item[Two-sector regime ($k < \kfs$):] $\Omega_{\rm m,growth} = 0.309$
    (both CDM and $\phiDM$ cluster). The Hubble function is
    $E^2(a) = 0.309\,a^{-3} + 0.840\,f_{\rm DE}(a)$
    with the Chevallier--Polarski--Linder \citep{ChevallierPolarski2001} form
    $f_{\rm DE}(a) = a^{-3(1+w_0+w_a)}\,e^{-3w_a(1-a)}$.
    This regime covers both RSD surveys ($k \sim 0.01$--$0.1\,h/\mathrm{Mpc}$)
    and the $\sigma_8$ scale ($k = 0.125\,h/\mathrm{Mpc}$).
\end{description}

We integrate the growth ODE on a \emph{flat} background ($\Omega_m = 0.309$,
$\Omega_{\rm DE} = 0.691$, so $E^2(a{=}1) = 1$) and compare to the
$\Lambda$CDM solution ($\Omega_m = 0.315$) to obtain the two-sector growth factor:
\begin{equation}
  G_{\rm 2s} = \frac{D_1^{\rm SSEE}(\Omega_m=0.309)}{D_1^{\Lambda{\rm CDM}}(\Omega_m=0.315)} = 1.003,
  \label{eq:G2s}
\end{equation}
which sets the growth rate $f = d\ln D/d\ln a$ for the redshift-space distortion
observable \citep{Percival2009} $\fsig(z) = f(z)\,\sigls\,D(z)/D(0)$.
The amplitude that normalises $\fsig$ is the \emph{large-scale clustering}
amplitude $\sigls = 0.811\times G_{\rm 2s} = 0.811\times1.003 = 0.814$: RSD
surveys probe $k\simeq 0.01$--$0.1\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$, well below the
free-streaming scale $k_{\rm fs} = 0.754\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$
(Section~\ref{subsec:sigma8}), where the $\phi$-DM sector still clusters as
cold matter --- so at RSD scales the two-sector growth is identical to the
single-sector ($\Omega_m = 0.309$) result. This is physically distinct from the
window-integrated amplitude $\sigeff = 0.747$ that enters $S_8$: the
$R = 8\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}$ top-hat support \emph{crosses} $k_{\rm fs}$ and
therefore feels the free-streaming suppression, which is read directly from the
two-sector \textsc{class} run with the particle's own relic temperature $T_\phi$
(Section~\ref{subsec:sigma8}). The $0.814\!\to\!0.747$ split is the physical
signature of free-streaming ---structure is erased on small (lensing) but not on
large (RSD) scales---not a free normalisation.

\subsection{$\sigma_8$ and $S_8$}
\label{subsec:sigma8}

The structure amplitude is not obtained by applying a growth factor to a
$\Lambda$CDM reference. It is read directly off a two-sector \textsc{class} run in
which the cold sector ($\Omega_{\rm cdm}=0.160$) clusters fully and the warm
$\phi$-DM sector ($\Omega_{\rm ncdm}=0.149$, $m_\phi=40.70$~eV, relic temperature
$T_\phi=0.539\,T_\nu$) is partially erased below its free-streaming scale. The
Boltzmann output gives:
\begin{equation}
  \sigeff = 0.747, \qquad
  S_8 \equiv \sigeff\,\sqrt{\Omega_{\rm m,total}/0.3}
       = 0.747 \times \sqrt{0.309/0.3} = 0.758.
  \label{eq:sigma8eff}
\end{equation}
The KiDS-1000 value $S_8 = 0.759 \pm 0.024$ \citep{KiDS2021} gives a tension of
$0.04\sigma$ --- the two-sector $\phi$-DM particle \emph{resolves} the $S_8$ lensing
tension, compared with $\sim3\sigma$ for $\Lambda$CDM and $3.5\sigma$ for the
single-sector SSEE baseline of Paper~5. The DES~Y3 value $S_8 = 0.776 \pm 0.017$
\citep{DESY32022} gives a tension of $0.96\sigma$
[$=(0.776-0.758)/\sqrt{0.017^2+0.005^2}$]. Both are achieved with zero quantities
fit to weak-lensing data: $m_\phi$ and $T_\phi$ are fixed upstream by the algebraic
chain and the relic constraint, and the suppression is whatever \textsc{class}
computes from them.

\paragraph{Free-streaming scale as a Boltzmann output.}
The small-scale suppression carried by the warm sector can be summarised by an
effective Viel+2005 transfer function
$T_{\rm WDM}(k) = [1+(\alpha k)^{2\mu}]^{-5/\mu}$ ($\mu=1.12$, Viel+2005)
mixed as $P_{\rm mix} = P_{\rm lin}\,[(1-f_\phi) + f_\phi T_{\rm WDM}]^2$. Crucially,
$\alpha$ is \emph{not} a free parameter calibrated to data: fitting the above form to
the ratio $P_{\rm particle}(k)/P_{\rm cold}(k)$ produced by the two \textsc{class}
runs returns
\begin{equation}
  \alpha = 1.117\,\mathrm{Mpc}/h \quad (\text{mixing fraction } f_\phi = 0.755),
  \label{eq:alpha_output}
\end{equation}
an \emph{output} of the Boltzmann integration with $m_\phi$ and $T_\phi$ held fixed.
This replaces the earlier procedure in which $\alpha$ was tuned to reproduce the
observed KiDS amplitude; here the amplitude is a prediction and $\alpha$ merely labels
its scale dependence.

\section{Bayesian MCMC Validation}
\label{sec:mcmc}

To complement the algebraic derivation with a Bayesian consistency test, we
perform an \texttt{emcee} \citep{emcee} MCMC in which the two-sector growth
observables are computed from a \emph{validated \textsc{class} emulator} rather
than a phenomenological transfer function. We build a $22\times20$ grid of
direct \textsc{class} two-sector runs over $(\Omega_{\varphi\mathrm{DM}},\,m_\phi)$
at the canonical $\omega_m$-direct background ($\Omega_{m,\mathrm{CMB}}=0.30889$,
$w_0=-0.840$, $w_a=-0.670$), extracting $\sigma_8(z)$ by the top-hat integral;
the primordial amplitude enters analytically ($\sigma_8\propto\sqrt{A_s}$, exact
in linear theory). The emulator reproduces direct \textsc{class} to a mean error
of $0.06\%$ (maximum $0.11\%$) at ten posterior samples. We float
$\theta = (\Omega_{\varphi\mathrm{DM}},\, m_\phi,\, A_s)$ with \emph{flat} priors
on the $\phi$-DM sector --- deliberately \emph{not} centred on the algebraic
values, so the data alone decide where the posterior lands --- and a Planck prior
on the amplitude ($10^9 A_s = 2.101\pm0.030$). Data: the six canonical $f\sigma_8$
surveys of Paper~5, the KiDS-1000 constraint $S_8 = 0.759\pm0.0225$
\citep{KiDS2021}, and DES Y3 $S_8 = 0.776\pm0.017$. The chain uses $N_w=60$
walkers $\times\,N_\mathrm{steps}=12000$ (burn-in 3000).

\subsection{KiDS--DES internal tension and its interpretation in \SSEE}
\label{subsec:des}

The forward two-sector \textsc{class} prediction $S_8 = 0.758$ agrees with both
the KiDS-1000 measurement $S_8 = 0.759 \pm 0.024$ \citep{KiDS2021}
($0.04\sigma$) and the DES Y3 measurement $S_8 = 0.776 \pm 0.017$
\citep{DESY32022} ($0.96\sigma$). Because the prediction lands between the two
surveys, the decomposition below clarifies which matter density enters the
$S_8$ statistic and why no survey is singled out as anomalous.

\paragraph{Structure of the $S_8$ statistic.}
The quantity $S_8 \equiv \sigma_8\sqrt{\Omega_m/0.3}$ is constructed to be
approximately orthogonal to the weak-lensing amplitude degeneracy direction.
The normalisation $\Omega_m = 0.3$ is conventional and implicitly assumes the
inferred $\Omega_m$ is close to $0.3$.  In the \SSEE\ framework the relevant
matter density has two distinct values: $\Omega_{m,\mathrm{dyn}} = 0.160$
(dynamical dark matter sector) and $\Omega_{m,\mathrm{CMB}} = 0.30889$
($=\omega_m/h^2$, $\omega_m$-direct; total including the $\phi$-DM clustering
contribution active at $k < k_{\rm fs}$).  Growth-rate observables probe $\Omega_{m,\mathrm{dyn}}$,
while the CMB acoustic geometry is sensitive to $\Omega_{m,\mathrm{CMB}}$.

\paragraph{Why KiDS and DES disagree internally.}
KiDS-1000 \citep{KiDS2021} and DES Y3 \citep{DESY32022} themselves differ by
$\Delta S_8 \approx 0.4\sigma$, which is sub-dominant but non-negligible.
This residual is driven by: (i) different angular scale cuts ($\ell_{\rm max}$);
(ii) differing baryonic feedback priors; and (iii) partially overlapping
shear calibration schemes.  These internal systematics shift $S_8$ at the
$\sim 1\%$ level — comparable to the statistical error — making a direct
sub-percent comparison to \SSEE\ premature.

\paragraph{The correct SSEE comparison.}
In the two-sector model the total matter clustering budget uses
$\Omega_m^{\rm tot} = \Omega_{\rm CDM} + \Omega_{\phi{\rm DM}} = 0.309$
when $k < k_{\rm fs}$.  With this total, the \SSEE\ $S_8$ statistic evaluates
to $S_{8,\rm SSEE} = \sigma_8^{\rm eff} \sqrt{0.309/0.3} = 0.758$, in agreement
with KiDS ($0.04\sigma$) and DES ($0.96\sigma$).
Neither survey is singled out as anomalous with respect to \SSEE;
the prediction lands inside both error bars.

The improvement over single-sector \SSEE\ (Paper~5 corrected: $\sigma_8 = 0.834$,
$S_8 = 0.846$, $3.5\sigma$ KiDS) is driven by the $\phi$-DM free-streaming
suppression of $\sigma_8^{\rm eff}$ to $0.747$ in the forward \textsc{class} run,
which collapses the KiDS $S_8$ tension from $3.5\sigma$ to $0.04\sigma$ with no
fitted parameter.  A joint DES+KiDS likelihood marginalised
over $(\sigma_8, \Omega_m)$ simultaneously — as will be provided by
Euclid Wide Survey weak lensing — will provide a definitive test.

\subsection{Compatibility with Lyman-$\alpha$ and WDM constraints}
\label{subsec:lya}

A potential objection is that $\mphi = 40.70\,\mathrm{eV}$ lies far below the
standard thermal-WDM Lyman-$\alpha$ bound $m_{\rm WDM} \gtrsim 3.3\text{--}3.5\,\mathrm{keV}$
\citep{Viel2005,Viel2013,PalanqueDelabrouille2020}.  We address this in three steps.

\paragraph{Step 1: $\phi$-DM is not thermal WDM.}
Thermal WDM bounds assume (a) 100\% of the dark matter is a single thermally
produced species, and (b) the momentum distribution is a Fermi-Dirac or
Bose-Einstein function at a single temperature.  Neither condition holds here.
The $\phi$-DM sector accounts for only half of the total matter budget
($\Omega_{\phiDM}/\Omega_{m}^{\rm tot} \simeq 0.50$).
The remaining sector is cold CDM ($\Omega_{\rm CDM} = 0.160$), which provides
full gravitational clustering at all scales $k < \kfs$ and is \emph{not}
suppressed.  For mixed dark matter models with a WDM fraction $f_{\rm WDM}$,
the Lyman-$\alpha$ constraint on the WDM component is substantially relaxed
(see e.g.\ \citealt{Boyarsky2019,Adhikari2017}); for $f_{\rm WDM} \approx 0.5$, the
effective bound weakens to $m_{\rm WDM}^{\rm eff} \gtrsim 0.1$--$0.5\,\mathrm{keV}$.

\paragraph{Step 2: The observable is $\kfs$, not $\mphi$ — and \textsc{class} computes it directly.}
The quantity constrained by Lyman-$\alpha$ forest measurements is the matter
power spectrum suppression wavenumber $\kfs$, not the particle mass directly.
For the canonical particle the relic temperature is not a free production knob:
it is fixed by the relic-abundance constraint for the hot sector,
\begin{equation}
  T_\phi = T_\nu \left(\frac{\Omega_{\phiDM}h^2 \times 93.14}{\mphi/\mathrm{eV}}\right)^{1/3}
         = 0.539\,T_\nu ,
  \label{eq:Tphi_relic}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the $\phi$-DM is \emph{colder} than a standard neutrino. With $T_\phi$
and $\mphi = 40.70\,\mathrm{eV}$ fixed, the suppression scale is then read
directly off the two-sector \textsc{class} run as the wavenumber where the
ratio $P_{\phi}/P_{\rm cold}$ falls to one half:
\begin{equation}
  k_{1/2}^{\textsc{class}} = 0.337\;h/\mathrm{Mpc},
  \label{eq:khalf_class}
\end{equation}
a Boltzmann output, independent of any thermal- or DW-relic fitting formula.
A complementary analytic estimate, using the non-relativistic free-streaming
form corrected for the colder relic temperature,
\begin{equation}
  \kfs \approx 0.018\,\sqrt{\Omega_m}\,\frac{\mphi}{\mathrm{eV}}\,
        \frac{T_\nu}{T_\phi} \approx 0.754\;h/\mathrm{Mpc},
  \label{eq:kfs_analytic}
\end{equation}
brackets the \textsc{class} half-mode scale at the order-of-magnitude level
(the two probe different points of the same transfer-function roll-off).

\textbf{Key point:} the suppression scale is no longer inferred from an
extrapolated relic formula. The canonical mass $\mphi = 40.70\,\mathrm{eV}$
(Section~\ref{sec:mass}) and the relic temperature $T_\phi = 0.539\,T_\nu$
together place the half-mode at $k_{1/2}^{\textsc{class}}\approx 0.337\,h/\mathrm{Mpc}$,
exactly in the window required to resolve the $\sigma_8$ and $f\sigma_8$
tensions, with the full erasure profile predicted by \textsc{class} rather than
by a calibrated fitting relation.

\textbf{Validity caveat.}  The analytic estimate (Eq.~\ref{eq:kfs_analytic})
uses the neutrino-like free-streaming scaling and should be read as an
order-of-magnitude cross-check; the authoritative suppression scale is the
\textsc{class} half-mode (Eq.~\ref{eq:khalf_class}).
A direct Lyman-$\alpha$ test should be formulated in terms of the predicted
suppression profile, not the thermal-WDM mass bound.

\paragraph{Step 3: What remains observable.}
At scales $k > k_{1/2}^{\textsc{class}} = 0.337\,h/\mathrm{Mpc}$ (relevant for the
Lyman-$\alpha$ forest at $z \sim 2$--$4$), the CDM sector still contributes
$(\Omega_{\rm CDM}/\Omega_m^{\rm tot}) \approx 50\%$ of the power.  Expanding
the mixing formula $P_{\rm mix}/P_{\rm \Lambda CDM} = [(1-\fphi) + \fphi\,T_{\rm WDM}]^2$
gives
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\Delta P(k)}{P_{\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}}} =
  -2\fphi(1-\fphi)(1-T_{\rm WDM})
  - \fphi^2\,(1-T_{\rm WDM})^2,
  \label{eq:power_suppression}
\end{equation}
with $\fphi \equiv f_{\phiDM} = 0.755$ (CLASS output).  In two asymptotic regimes:
\begin{alignat}{2}
  k \ll \kfs:& \quad T_{\rm WDM}\to 1,\quad
    &\frac{\Delta P}{P} &\to 0, \\
  k \gg \kfs:& \quad T_{\rm WDM}\to 0,\quad
    &\frac{\Delta P}{P} &\to -\fphi(2-\fphi) \simeq -0.95.
    \label{eq:suppression_asymp}
\end{alignat}
At the CLASS half-mode $k_{1/2}^{\textsc{class}} = 0.337\,h/\mathrm{Mpc}$ the
mixed transfer function reaches $T_{\rm WDM}\approx 0.19$, giving
$\Delta P/P \approx -0.65$, i.e.\ $65\%$ suppression at that scale.
The mixing fraction $\fphi = f_{\phiDM} = 0.755$ is itself a CLASS output
(Eq.~\ref{eq:alpha_output}), not a free parameter.
The dominant contribution is the linear term $-2\fphi(1-\fphi)(1-T_{\rm WDM})$;
the quadratic $-\fphi^2(1-T_{\rm WDM})^2$ adds the remainder.
A dedicated Lyman-$\alpha$ likelihood analysis
(planned with the IS-EFT framework) is required to verify that this
suppression profile is consistent with observed forest data.  Such analysis
constitutes a \emph{falsification test}: if the forest shows no suppression
in the decade $0.3 \lesssim k \lesssim 0.7\,h/\mathrm{Mpc}$, the two-sector
model is excluded.

The observational predictions of this paper — the resolved $S_8$ tension
($\sigeff = 0.747$, $S_8 = 0.758$ at $0.04\sigma$ from KiDS) and the proposed
$\fsig$ resolution — emerge from the forward two-sector \textsc{class} run, in
which the particle's own free-streaming sets the small-scale suppression.

\subsection{Physical origin of the mass relation}
\label{subsec:origin}

The forward prediction $\mphi = \mnu^{\rm active}\times(\mathrm{SOLAR}^2\cdot\mathrm{KRYSTOS})$
multiplies an energy scale by a \emph{pure} $(\varphi,\pi)$ number, so it is
dimensionally consistent and writes directly into the free-scalar action of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:phiDM_lagrangian} (Section~\ref{subsec:lagrangian}). We note the
status of each ingredient:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\mnu^{\rm active} = 0.0685\,\mathrm{eV}$ is the active neutrino scale
    $R_2\times0.9530\,\mathrm{eV}$ \citep{Lesgourgues2006}, with
    $R_2=\Omega_{\rm DNAV}/(\KAL\cdot T)=0.07188$ fixed upstream by the density
    chain (Section~\ref{subsec:mass_derivation}); it sets the energy unit.
  \item The multiplier $\mathrm{SOLAR}^2\cdot\mathrm{KRYSTOS} = 594.28$ is a
    dimensionless number built entirely from $\varphi$ and $\pi$. It is the
    structural enhancement that lifts the warm relic from the neutrino scale to
    $\sim$\,37 eV, and \emph{is} the mass coefficient of the explicit quadratic
    term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:phiDM_lagrangian}.
  \item Because an energy times a pure number returns an energy, there is no
    hidden unit conversion. This is the decisive break from the earlier
    $\mphi=\mnu\times\Halg$ pattern, in which $\Halg=3(\varphi+\pi)^2$ carried
    Hubble units and only ``worked'' by stripping them off to read $67.96$ as a
    bare number. That units-broken coincidence is retired; the present relation
    is a genuine forward prediction with zero quantities fit to data.
\end{enumerate}

Writing the coefficient into a Lagrangian \emph{closes the incompleteness} that
previously affected OP-9: the mass parameter no longer floats as a units-broken
coincidence but is the mass term of an explicit free-scalar action. What remains
open --- tracked as a refined OP-9 --- is the deeper UV origin of the specific
multiplier $\mathrm{SOLAR}^2\cdot\mathrm{KRYSTOS}$, in parallel with the origin of
the $\Sigma m_\nu$ scale itself (OP-14). The physical reading is that the
$\phi$-DM field acquires its mass from the same $(\varphi,\pi)$ structural algebra
that fixes the neutrino scale; as established in Section~\ref{subsec:production},
$\phi$-DM is \emph{not} a sterile neutrino.
The field content is fixed by the action~\eqref{eq:phiDM_lagrangian}: $\phiDM$
is a real scalar boson, populated by gravitational particle production rather
than by active--sterile mixing.  The remaining open item is the \emph{ab initio}
relic abundance integral, not the field's identity.

\subsection{Status of the parameter count in the two-sector extension}
\label{subsec:zero_params}

The \SSEE\ philosophy requires that no number be \emph{fitted} to data. We
distinguish here between \emph{not fitted to data} (i.e., fixed prior to the
comparison) and \emph{derived from first principles}. The two-sector observables
of this paper are not fitted; their inputs include three phenomenological
elements explicitly tracked as open problems:

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Omega_{\phiDM} = (\MIRA - 1)\,\Omega_{\rm m,dyn}$: derived from the
    MIRA algebraic identity. \emph{Not fitted.} (MIRA itself has an algebraic
    value $(3\varphi+\pi)/4$ but no derived dynamical mechanism — OP-8.)
  \item $\mphi = \mnu^{\rm active}\,(\mathrm{SOLAR}^2\cdot\mathrm{KRYSTOS})
    = 40.70\,\mathrm{eV}$: a zero-fitting forward prediction. The multiplier
    $\mathrm{SOLAR}^2\cdot\mathrm{KRYSTOS} = 594.28$ is a pure $(\varphi,\pi)$
    number and is the mass term of the written scalar Lagrangian
    (Section~\ref{subsec:origin}), so the mass is dimensionally consistent.
    \emph{Not fitted.} What remains open is the UV origin of the multiplier
    (refined OP-9); $\Sigma m_\nu^{\rm active}$ is Type~P (OP-14).
  \item $\xi$: non-minimal coupling, currently treated as a free parameter
    (OP-11). Constrained below by the production mechanism.
  \item $\sigeff = 0.747$ and $S_8 = 0.758$: \textsc{class} outputs of the
    two-sector run (cold + $\phi$-DM ncdm), with the suppression scale emerging
    from the Boltzmann hierarchy. \emph{Not fitted} — $S_8$ lands at $0.04\sigma$
    from KiDS as a forward prediction, not by tuning.
\end{itemize}

The two-sector extension does not introduce new \emph{fitted} parameters
relative to the background sector. The mass $m_\phi$ is no longer a loose
ansatz: its coefficient is the mass term of a written scalar Lagrangian, which
closes the dimensional-incompleteness of OP-9 and leaves only the UV origin of
the multiplier $\mathrm{SOLAR}^2\cdot\mathrm{KRYSTOS}$ open. The remaining
phenomenological inputs are the non-minimal coupling $\xi$ (OP-11) and the UV
origin of the multiplier (refined OP-9). (The former MIRA matter mechanism,
OP-8, is dissolved under the $\omega_m$-direct reframe: there is no matter
factor to derive.) Resolution of OP-9/11 — deriving the multiplier and the
coupling from first principles within the IS-EFT framework — would restore a
strict zero-parameter status. This is left for future work.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We have proposed a phenomenological two-sector resolution for the $f\sigma_8$ growth-rate tension identified in Paper~5 of the
\SSEE\ series. The key results are:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{EFT scan}: kinetic braiding ($\alpha_B$) is a no-go — it suppresses
    growth rather than enhancing it. Run-rate modification ($\alpha_M = -0.08$)
    reduces the tension to $1.57\sigma$ but requires tuning to the observational
    bound.

  \item \textbf{Two-sector model}: the MIRA algebraic identity
    $\MIRA \approx 2$ implies a second dark matter sector,
    $\Omega_{\phiDM} = \Omega_{\rm m,CMB}-\Omega_{\rm m,dyn} = 0.30889 - 0.160 = 0.14889$, with a
    free-streaming cutoff at $\kfs$.

  \item \textbf{Mass from a written Lagrangian}:
    $\mphi = \mnu^{\rm active}\,(\mathrm{SOLAR}^2\cdot\mathrm{KRYSTOS})
    = 0.0685 \times 594.28 = 40.70$~eV, with the multiplier a pure
    $(\varphi,\pi)$ number that is the mass term of the scalar Lagrangian
    $\mathcal{L}_\Phi = \tfrac12(\partial\Phi)^2 - \tfrac12 m_\phi^2 \Phi^2$
    (Section~\ref{subsec:origin}). This is dimensionally consistent and closes
    the incompleteness of OP-9; what remains open is the UV origin of the
    multiplier (refined OP-9), with $\Sigma m_\nu^{\rm active}$ Type~P (OP-14).
    Not fitted to growth data.

  \item \textbf{Verification (\textsc{class}, zero fitting)}: the two-sector run
    gives $\sigeff = 0.747$ and $S_8 = 0.758$ ($0.04\sigma$ KiDS,
    \emph{resolving} the lensing tension vs.\ $\Lambda$CDM $\sim3\sigma$) and
    $f\sigma_8$ mean tension $\mathbf{0.93\sigma}$
    (single-sector baseline: $0.70\sigma$; the free-streaming that resolves $S_8$
    raises this modestly, still $<1\sigma$, close to $\Lambda$CDM
    $0.73\sigma$). The CMB acoustic peaks ($\ell = 219/533/807$) and RMS$(TT)
    = 0.93\%$ vs.\ $\Lambda$CDM are conserved because $m_\phi \approx 37$~eV is
    non-relativistic well before recombination. All background tests — BAO
    ($\chi^2_r = 0.01$), clusters ($\chi^2_r = 0.122$) — conserved.
\end{enumerate}

The framework \SSEE-V3.6 now offers a unified linear-level description across six categories of observational tests
(background expansion, CMB, BAO, galaxy clusters, weak lensing, and RSD growth
rate). The lensing tension is \emph{resolved} at the linear level by the
two-sector model evaluated directly in \textsc{class} ($S_8 = 0.758$ at
$0.04\sigma$ from KiDS), with no warm-dark-matter transfer function fitted to
data: the suppression scale is a Boltzmann output. The mass coupling
$m_\phi = \Sigma m_\nu^{\rm active}\,(\mathrm{SOLAR}^2\cdot\mathrm{KRYSTOS})$ is
now the mass term of a written scalar Lagrangian; the free-streaming scale
$k_{\rm fs}$ still awaits verification against Lyman-$\alpha$ forest data. The
remaining background tension ($H(z)$, $\chi^2_r = 1.861$) is structural and
traceable to $\Omega_{\rm m,dyn} = 0.160$.

The \textbf{falsifiable predictions} of this paper are: (1) the $\phi$-dark
matter free-streaming scale $\kfs = 0.754\,h/$Mpc (with a \textsc{class}-measured
half-mode at $k_{1/2} = 0.337\,h/$Mpc), set by the mass
$\mphi = 40.70\,\mathrm{eV}$ and probed by the Lyman-$\alpha$ forest and by
DESI~Y3 / Euclid galaxy clustering (2026--2028) --- the relevant channel
because $\phiDM$ is a gravitationally produced scalar with no Standard-Model
portal (Section~\ref{subsec:production}), and so leaves no neutrino-sector
signature; and (2) $\sigeff = 0.747$ ($S_8 = 0.758$), testable by Euclid
weak-lensing (2026--2028).
