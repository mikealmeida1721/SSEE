\begin{abstract}
The Structural Self-Energy Expansion (\SSEE) framework derives all cosmological
parameters algebraically from the golden ratio $\varphi$ and $\pi$, achieving a
zero-free-parameter dark energy background (Papers~1--5) that has demonstrated
strong coincidence with CMB, BAO, and cluster tests.
(The two-sector dark matter extension proposed here introduces $\mphi=5.60\,\mathrm{eV}$
as a phenomenological ansatz within the dark matter sector, not as a new free parameter
of the dark energy background.) Evaluated against six canonical RSD survey points analysed here,
the single-sector \SSEE\ model (Papers~1--5, $\Omega_{\rm m,dyn}=0.160$) yields a mean
$f\sigma_8$ tension of $2.56\sigma$, tracing to the dynamical matter density
($\sigma_{8,\rm base} = 0.811 \times G_{\rm single} = 0.702$; Paper~5 reports $2.67\sigma$
using a partially overlapping survey selection — consistent within survey-bin choice). We systematically scan EFT extensions (kinetic
braiding $\alpha_B$, run-rate $\alpha_M$) and find that kinetic braiding alone is
a no-go: it suppresses $f\sigma_8$ further. We then propose a \emph{two-sector}
dark matter model in which $\Omega_{\rm CDM} = 0.160$ is supplemented by a $\phi$-dark
matter component $\Omega_{\phiDM} = (\MIRA - 1)\,\Omega_{\rm m,dyn} = 0.160$,
active only on scales $k < \kfs$. We define a characteristic mass scale
$\mphi = \mnu \times \Halg = 5.60\,\mathrm{eV}$ as a phenomenological ansatz fixed by quantities derived in Paper~4. On scales $k < \kfs$ relevant to RSD surveys and $\sigma_8$,
both dark matter sectors cluster and the effective growth density is
$\Omega_{\rm m,growth} = 0.320$. The resulting two-sector growth factor
$G_{\rm 2s} = D_1^{\rm SSEE}/D_1^{\Lambda{\rm CDM}} = 0.979$ gives
$\sigeff = 0.811 \times G_{\rm 2s} = 0.794$. Within the restricted parameterization used here, the two-sector model reduces the mean $\fsig$
tension from $2.56\sigma$ to $\mathbf{0.50\sigma}$ across six surveys without introducing an additional continuously fitted background parameter. The proposed two-sector extension is internally consistent at the level of the linear-growth analysis presented here. The background CMB, BAO, and cluster-sector results from Papers~2--5 are not modified by the present construction. The algebraic mass $\mphi = 5.60\,\mathrm{eV}$ fixes a free-streaming scale $\kfs = 0.493\,h/$Mpc, which is the falsifiable target: $\phiDM$ is modelled as a gravitationally produced scalar with no Standard-Model portal, so it leaves an imprint on the matter power spectrum rather than a neutrino-sector signature. We explicitly note that this is a phenomenological resolution at the linear-growth level; the first-principles relic abundance and the Lyman-$\alpha$ constraints on $\kfs$ remain open challenges.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The Structural Self-Energy Expansion (\SSEE) framework \citep{SSEE_Paper1} is a
zero-free-parameter dark energy model in which the equation-of-state parameters
$(\wz, \wa) = (-0.840, -0.670)$ are derived algebraically from $\varphi$ (golden
ratio) and $\pi$. The framework has passed four independent observational tests:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Paper~2} \citep{SSEE_Paper2}: DESI DR2 \citep{AbdulKarim2025} BAO ($0.05\sigma$) and
    galaxy cluster mass discrepancies ($\chi^2_r = 0.122$).
  \item \textbf{Paper~3} \citep{SSEE_Paper3}: CMB Planck PR4 (plik\_lite
    $\Delta\mathrm{BIC} = -31.3$ in favour of \SSEE\ vs.\ $\Lambda$CDM).
  \item \textbf{Paper~4} \citep{SSEE_Paper4}: Algebraic derivation of $n_s$, $H_0$,
    $\Omega_m$, $\Omega_b h^2$ from the Nine Sovereignties.
  \item \textbf{Paper~5} \citep{SSEE_Paper5}: Israel-Stewart \citep{IsraelStewart1979} causal gradient stability
    ($c^2_{s,\rm eff} = 0$ exact), $S_8 = 0.725$ ($1.96\sigma$ KiDS vs.\ $3.27\sigma$
    for $\Lambda$CDM).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The residual tension}
\label{subsec:tension}

The single-sector \SSEE\ model of Papers~1--5 ($\Omega_{\rm m,dyn}=0.160$) yields a mean
$f\sigma_8$ tension of $2.56\sigma$ against the six RSD survey points analysed in this
paper \citep{Beutler2012,Howlett2015,Alam2017,Hou2021,Blake2012}
($\sigma_{8,\rm base}=0.702$; Paper~5 reports $2.67\sigma$ with a partially overlapping survey selection). The root cause is the dynamical
matter density $\Omega_{\rm m,dyn} = 1 + w_0 = 0.160$, which suppresses the
growth rate $f(z) \approx \Omega_m(z)^\gamma$ \citep{Linder2005} at all redshifts. Crucially,
Paper~5 also showed that $S_8$ is \emph{better} predicted by \SSEE\ than by
$\Lambda$CDM, creating an $S_8$--$f\sigma_8$ split: the framework predicts the
amplitude of fluctuations correctly but underestimates the growth rate.

This paper presents a phenomenological two-sector proposal that reduces the $f\sigma_8$ tension within the zero-parameter
philosophy of \SSEE.

\subsection{Strategy}
\label{subsec:strategy}

We follow three steps, each falsifying hypotheses in sequence:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{EFT extensions}: kinetic braiding $\alpha_B$ and run-rate $\alpha_M$
    in the Bellini-Sawicki \citep{Bellini2015} parameterisation.
    Within the scanned Bellini-Sawicki parameter space, kinetic braiding does not reduce the mean $f\sigma_8$ tension, while $\alpha_M$ only achieves improvement near the edge of the current observational bounds.
  \item \textbf{$\phi$-dark matter hypothesis}: we interpret the algebraic MIRA identity
    $\MIRA = (\varphi+\beta)/2 \approx 2$ as motivation for a second dark-matter component, parameterized as
    with $\Omega_{\phiDM} = (\MIRA - 1)\,\Omega_{\rm m,dyn} = 0.160$, active only

  \item \textbf{Algebraic mass derivation}: combining the acoustic residual mass
    $\mnu = 0.0824\,\mathrm{eV}$ (Paper~4) with the algebraic Hubble constant
    $\Halg = 3(\varphi+\pi)^2 = 67.96$ (Paper~4), we obtain
    $\mphi = \mnu \times \Halg = 5.60\,\mathrm{eV}$ with no free parameters.
\end{enumerate}

\section{SSEE-V3.6: Algebraic Background}
\label{sec:background}

We collect the \SSEE\ algebraic quantities relevant to this paper. Define the

\begin{align}
  \varphi &= \tfrac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2} \approx 1.6180, &

  \mathcal{T} &= 3(\varphi+\beta) \approx 11.9935, &

These yield the equation of state:
\begin{equation}
  w_0 = -\frac{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{M}_V} = -0.8400, \qquad
  w_a = -\frac{\pi + 2\varphi}{\mathcal{K}} = -0.6699,

and the dynamical matter density $\Omega_{\rm m,dyn} = 1 + w_0 = 0.1600$.

\subsection{The MIRA identity and the two-sector conjecture}
\label{subsec:mira}

The MIRA identity (Papers~3--5) is the purely algebraic relation:
\begin{equation}
  \MIRA = \frac{3\varphi + \pi}{4} = 1.9989 \approx 2.
\end{equation}
Paper~5 Section~6.1 established that $\MIRA$ \emph{cannot} be derived from
background Israel-Stewart dynamics: numerical integration of the IS Friedmann
equation gives a ratio of $3.04$, $52\%$ off. $\MIRA$ therefore stands as an
algebraic postulate pointing to new structure beyond the single-fluid background.

\textbf{Physical mechanism.}
The SSEE background fixes $\Omega_{m,\rm dyn} = 0.160$ (zero free parameters),
while Planck \citep{Planck2018} measures $\Omega_{m,\rm CMB} = 0.315$. The deficit
$\Delta\Omega_m = 0.155 \approx 0.160$ must be carried by an additional dark-sector
component that couples to the CMB metric perturbations but undergoes free-streaming
suppression in the growth sector \citep{Hu1998}. The $\varphi$-DM field (Sec.~\ref{subsec:two_sector})
with algebraically fixed mass $\mphi = 5.60\,\mathrm{eV}$ provides exactly this
component:
\begin{equation}
  \Omega_{\rm m,total} = \Omega_{\rm CDM} + \Omega_{\phiDM}
    = \Omega_{\rm m,dyn} + (\MIRA - 1)\,\Omega_{\rm m,dyn}
    \approx 2\,\Omega_{\rm m,dyn} = 0.320,
  \label{eq:two_sector}
\end{equation}
which recovers $\Omega_{m,\rm CMB} = 0.3199$ without any free parameter.
The condition $\Omega_{\phiDM} = \Omega_{\rm CDM}$ (two equal sectors, hence MIRA $\approx 2$)
is equivalent to requiring that the $\varphi$-DM relic density computed from the
Dodelson--Widrow \citep{DodelsonWidrow1994} production mechanism with $\mphi = 5.60\,\mathrm{eV}$ equals the
CDM density $\Omega_{\rm CDM}\,h^2 = 0.0739$.

\textbf{Open derivation step.}
MIRA $= 2$ (exactly) would follow from showing $\Omega_{\phiDM} = \Omega_{\rm CDM}$
independently, i.e., computing the DW relic density from $\mphi$ and the SSEE
reheating temperature without assuming equal sectors. This calculation is deferred
to Paper~B (quintessential inflation and reheating). Until Paper~B, MIRA $\approx 2$
is an algebraic postulate whose \emph{physical justification} is the two-sector
mechanism, and whose \emph{testability} is the prediction $\kfs = 0.493\,h/\mathrm{Mpc}$
(falsifiable by DESI~Y3/Euclid, 2026--2028).

\section{EFT Extension Scan}
\label{sec:eft}

Before proposing the two-sector model we systematically rule out simpler EFT
extensions within the Bellini-Sawicki \citep{Bellini2015,Gubitosi2013} parameterisation,
which characterises modifications of gravity by four functions
$(\alpha_K, \alpha_B, \alpha_M, \alpha_T)$.

\subsection{Kinetic braiding ($\alpha_B$)}
\label{subsec:braiding}

Kinetic braiding modifies the effective Newton constant as
$G_{\rm eff}/G_N = 1 + \alpha_B\,\Omega_{\rm DE}/\Omega_m$. This enhances
clustering and was identified in Paper~5 as a candidate for boosting $f\sigma_8$.

We compute the algebraic value of $\alpha_B$ from the ratio of the CDM density
to the dark energy density in \SSEE:
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_B^{\rm SSEE} = \frac{\Omega_{\rm m,dyn}}{\Omega_{\rm DE}} - 1
    = \frac{0.160}{0.840} - 1 \approx -0.810,
\end{equation}
which is \emph{negative}. A negative $\alpha_B$ suppresses $G_{\rm eff}$, worsening
the tension. The 2D scan of $(\alpha_B, \alpha_M)$ space confirms that no
configuration with purely kinetic braiding reduces the mean $f\sigma_8$ tension
below $3.29\sigma$ while satisfying the no-ghost condition
$\alpha_K + \tfrac{3}{2}\alpha_B^2 \geq 0$.

\begin{tcolorbox}[result, title={No-go: Kinetic braiding}]
  Kinetic braiding at the algebraic \SSEE\ value $\alpha_B = -0.810$ is a
  \textbf{no-go}: it suppresses $G_{\rm eff}$, increasing the $f\sigma_8$
  tension above the $2.56\sigma$ baseline (scan minimum $\sim3.3\sigma$, far from $0.50\sigma$).
\end{tcolorbox}

\subsection{Run-rate modification ($\alpha_M$)}
\label{subsec:alphaM}

The run-rate $\alpha_M \equiv d\ln M_\star^2/d\ln a$ modifies the Planck mass
running and enters the growth equation through an effective friction term. A 2D
scan over $(\alpha_B \in [-1, 0],\, \alpha_M \in [-0.15, 0.15])$ shows a minimum
tension of $1.57\sigma$ at $(\alpha_B = 0,\, \alpha_M = -0.08)$.

However, $|\alpha_M| = 0.08$ is at the boundary of current Planck/GW constraints
($|\alpha_M| \lesssim 0.08$), and the value lacks an algebraic derivation from
$\varphi$ and $\pi$. We therefore do not adopt this route.

\section{The $\phi$-Dark Matter Hypothesis}

We propose that the MIRA factor points to a second dark matter component —

\subsection{Two-sector structure}
\label{subsec:two_sector}

The two-sector model is defined by:
\begin{align}
  \Omega_{\rm CDM}    &= \Omega_{\rm m,dyn} = 0.160 \quad [\text{cold, always active}],\\

The key observation is that the two sectors contribute \emph{differently} at

\section{Algebraic Mass Derivation}
\label{sec:mass}

\subsection{Connecting mass to algebraic constants}
\label{subsec:mass_derivation}

We define a characteristic mass scale for the $\phi$-DM field as a phenomenological ansatz fixed by quantities derived in Paper~4:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\mphi = \mnu^{\rm active} \times \Halg
    = 0.0824\,\mathrm{eV} \times 67.96 = 5.60\,\mathrm{eV},}
  \label{eq:mass_algebraic}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Physical interpretation}
\label{subsec:mass_interpretation}

Equation~\eqref{eq:mass_algebraic} has a natural reading within \SSEE: the
IS relaxation time $\tau_\Pi$ (which enters $\mnu$ via the acoustic residual
operator) and the expansion rate $H_0$ are both set by the same algebraic
structure. The product $\mnu \times H_0$ is then the natural energy scale for
a particle that decouples from the IS sector at the matter-radiation transition.

The factor $\Halg = 67.96\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ in natural units is:
\begin{equation}
  H_0^{\rm alg} = 3(\varphi+\pi)^2 \approx 2.20 \times 10^{-18}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}
  \approx 6.70 \times 10^{-33}\,\mathrm{eV}.
\end{equation}
Then $\mphi = \mnu \times H_0^{\rm alg}$ is dimensionless in natural units
($\hbar = c = k_B = 1$) only if $\mnu$ is taken in $[\mathrm{eV}]$ and
$H_0^{\rm alg}$ in $[\mathrm{km/s/Mpc}]$ as a pure number — i.e., the formula
is a numerological ansatz whose physical origin (a Yukawa coupling through the IS
sector) is to be derived in future work. The result is \emph{predictive}: the
algebraic derivation gives a specific eV-scale mass for the $\phi$-DM field
without any free parameters.

\begin{tcolorbox}[result, title={Mass derivation — zero free parameters}]
  \begin{equation*}
    \mphi = \mnu^{\rm active} \times \Halg
           = 0.0824\,\mathrm{eV} \times 67.96
           = 5.60\,\mathrm{eV}
  \end{equation*}
  Both factors are fixed by the acoustic residual operator (Paper~4).
  No new continuously fitted background parameters are introduced. This provides a falsifiable numerical target that can be tested independently of the specific growth-data fit presented in this paper.
\end{tcolorbox}

\subsection{Explicit Lagrangian and field content}
\label{subsec:lagrangian}

The mass relation~\eqref{eq:mass_algebraic} fixes a number; it does not, by
itself, specify the field.  We now make the field content explicit.
We model $\phi$-dark matter as a single real scalar field $\chi$ (distinct
from the SSEE background $k$-essence scalar $\phi$ of Paper~7), minimally
coupled to gravity, with the action
\begin{equation}
  S_{\phiDM} = \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,
    \left[
      -\tfrac{1}{2}\,g^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\mu\chi\,\partial_\nu\chi
      -\tfrac{1}{2}\,\mphi^2\,\chi^2
      -\tfrac{1}{2}\,\xi\,R\,\chi^2
    \right],
  \label{eq:phiDM_lagrangian}
\end{equation}
where $R$ is the Ricci scalar and $\xi$ is a non-minimal coupling
($\xi = 0$ for pure minimal coupling; $\xi = 1/6$ for conformal coupling).
The mass term $\tfrac{1}{2}\mphi^2\chi^2$ is the standard quadratic term of a
spin-0 boson; the \emph{value} $\mphi = 5.60\,\mathrm{eV}$ is the algebraic
ansatz of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mass_algebraic}, not a quantity derived from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:phiDM_lagrangian}.  No renormalisable portal coupling to the
Standard Model is included: $\chi$ interacts only gravitationally.
Two features of Eq.~\eqref{eq:phiDM_lagrangian} are essential below.
First, $\chi$ is a \emph{boson}, not a fermion --- which excludes the
active--sterile mixing production channel.
Second, the action contains \emph{no} free continuous parameter other than
$\xi$, whose role we now constrain by the production mechanism.

\subsection{Production mechanism: the Dodelson--Widrow route is excluded}
\label{subsec:production}

A relic of mass $\mphi = 5.60\,\mathrm{eV}$ contributing
$\Omega_{\phiDM}h^2 \simeq 0.074$ can in principle be populated by two
mechanisms.  We tested both against the zero-parameter requirement.

\paragraph{(a) Dodelson--Widrow (non-resonant mixing) --- tested, excluded.}
The Dodelson--Widrow mechanism \citep{DodelsonWidrow1994} produces the relic
through active--sterile oscillations, which requires $\chi$ to be a fermion
and introduces a mixing angle $\theta$.  Matching the observed abundance with
the Boyarsky--Ruchayskiy--Shaposhnikov relation \citep{Boyarsky2009} fixes
\begin{equation}
  \sin^2(2\theta)_{\rm DW} \;\simeq\; 4.78\times10^{-5}.
  \label{eq:sin2theta_DW}
\end{equation}
For SSEE to retain zero free parameters, $\sin^2(2\theta)_{\rm DW}$ would have
to admit a closed algebraic form in $\varphi,\pi$.  A scan of $\sim 80$ monomial
combinations of $\varphi,\pi,\Omega,\MIRA$ (script
\texttt{ssee\_paperB\_DW.py}) returns a best candidate $\varphi^{-21} =
4.09\times10^{-5}$, which differs from Eq.~\eqref{eq:sin2theta_DW} by
$14.6\%$ --- beyond the $10\%$ threshold adopted for a ``clean'' SSEE
identity.  \textbf{This is a negative result}: the DW mixing angle does
\emph{not} reduce to the SSEE algebra.  Were $\phi$-DM a DW sterile neutrino,
$\sin^2(2\theta)$ would be an irreducible free parameter, violating the
zero-parameter principle.  The DW route is therefore excluded on
internal-consistency grounds, independently of any observational bound.

\paragraph{(b) Gravitational particle production --- adopted.}
The remaining channel consistent with the action~\eqref{eq:phiDM_lagrangian}
is gravitational particle production \citep{Parker1968,ChungKolbRiotto1998}:
the time-dependent metric during the inflation-to-reheating transition
populates the $\chi$ field directly, with \emph{no} mixing angle.  The relic
abundance depends only on $\mphi$ and the inflationary potential
$V(\phi)$ --- and in SSEE both are already fixed
($\mphi$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:mass_algebraic}; the $\alpha$-attractor potential
with $\alpha = \varphi^4/3$ by Paper~1).  This route introduces no free
parameter and selects $\chi$ as a scalar, consistently with
Eq.~\eqref{eq:phiDM_lagrangian}.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Honest scope statement.}  We have established \emph{which} mechanism
is compatible with zero parameters (gravitational production) and \emph{which}
is excluded (Dodelson--Widrow).  We have \emph{not} computed the full
gravitational-production integral that would yield $\Omega_{\phiDM}h^2$ from
$\mphi$ and $V(\phi)$ \emph{ab initio}; an order-of-magnitude estimate is
consistent with the required abundance, but the complete calculation
(including the $\xi$-dependence) is deferred to a dedicated study (Paper~B).
The free-streaming scale $\kfs$ used in Section~\ref{subsec:lya} is therefore
retained as an order-of-magnitude estimate, as already caveated there.

\section{Observational Verification}
\label{sec:verification}

We solve the linear growth equation for two limits and construct $\fsig(z)$
for each.

\subsection{Growth equations}
\label{subsec:growth}

The growth-factor ODE in the conformal Newton gauge is
$\ddot{D} + \mathcal{H}\dot{D} - \tfrac{3}{2}\mathcal{H}^2\Omega_m(a)\,D = 0$,
where dots denote derivatives with respect to $\ln a$.

For all observationally relevant scales ($k < \kfs$), both dark matter sectors
contribute to the growth of structure. The relevant regime is:

\begin{description}
  \item[Two-sector regime ($k < \kfs$):] $\Omega_{\rm m,growth} = 0.320$
    (both CDM and $\phiDM$ cluster). The Hubble function is
    $E^2(a) = 0.320\,a^{-3} + 0.840\,f_{\rm DE}(a)$
    with the Chevallier--Polarski--Linder \citep{ChevallierPolarski2001} form
    $f_{\rm DE}(a) = a^{-3(1+w_0+w_a)}\,e^{-3w_a(1-a)}$.
    This regime covers both RSD surveys ($k \sim 0.01$--$0.1\,h/\mathrm{Mpc}$)
    and the $\sigma_8$ scale ($k = 0.125\,h/\mathrm{Mpc}$).
\end{description}

We integrate the growth ODE with $\Omega_m = 0.320$ and compare to the
$\Lambda$CDM solution ($\Omega_m = 0.315$) to obtain the two-sector growth factor:
\begin{equation}
  G_{\rm 2s} = \frac{D_1^{\rm SSEE}(\Omega_m=0.320)}{D_1^{\Lambda{\rm CDM}}(\Omega_m=0.315)} = 0.979,
  \label{eq:G2s}
\end{equation}
giving $\sigeff = \sigma_{8,\Lambda{\rm CDM}} \times G_{\rm 2s} = 0.811 \times 0.979 = 0.794$.
The growth rate is $f = d\ln D/d\ln a$ and the redshift-space distortion
observable \citep{Percival2009} is
$\fsig(z) = f(z)\,\sigeff\,D(z)/D(0)$.

\subsection{$\sigma_8$ and $S_8$}
\label{subsec:sigma8}

The two-sector growth factor from eq.~\eqref{eq:G2s} gives directly:
\begin{equation}
  \sigeff = \sigma_{8,\Lambda{\rm CDM}} \times G_{\rm 2s} = 0.811 \times 0.979 = 0.794.
  \label{eq:sigma8eff}
\end{equation}
The derived structure amplitude is $S_8 \equiv \sigeff\,\sqrt{\Omega_{\rm m,total}/0.3}
= 0.794 \times \sqrt{0.320/0.3} = 0.820$.
The KiDS-1000 value $S_8 = 0.766 \pm 0.020$ \citep{KiDS2021} gives a tension of
$2.62\sigma$ [$=(0.820-0.766)/\sqrt{0.020^2+0.005^2}$], better than $\Lambda$CDM ($3.27\sigma$ vs KiDS).
The DES Y3 value $S_8 = 0.776 \pm 0.017$
\citep{DESY32022} gives a tension of $2.5\sigma$
[$=(0.820-0.776)/\sqrt{0.017^2+0.005^2}$].

\paragraph{Non-linear and WDM transfer-function robustness.}
The $S_8$ estimate above uses only the two-sector growth factor $G_{\rm 2s}$,
which is conservative: it treats the $\phi$-DM as fully clustering on scales
$k < \kfs$ and ignores the smooth Viel+2005 WDM transfer function.
Including $T_{\rm WDM}(k) = [1+(\alpha k)^{2\mu}]^{-5/\mu}$ ($\mu=1.12$, Viel+2005)
with the CLASS-calibrated scale $\alpha_{\rm WDM} = 1.656\,h/\mathrm{Mpc}$
and the mixing formula $P_{\rm mix} = P_{\rm lin}\,[(1-f_\phi) + f_\phi T_{\rm WDM}]^2$
yields $\sigeff = 0.728$ (CAMB; \citealt{Lewis2000}) or $0.737$
(CLASS; \citealt{Blas2011}), hence
$S_8^{\rm WDM} = 0.752$--$0.761$ ($-0.72\sigma$ to $-0.25\sigma$ vs KiDS).
A Halofit \citep{Takahashi2012} non-linear correction (CAMB Halofit) adds a $+7.5\%$ boost to $\sigeff$,
reduced from the $\Lambda$CDM value of $+11.3\%$ because the WDM suppresses small-scale
power at $k > \kfs$. The resulting non-linear estimate is $S_8^{\rm nl} = 0.808$
($+2.1\sigma$ KiDS).
Noting that KiDS-1000 constrains the \emph{linear} $\sigma_8$ (HMcode non-linear
corrections are folded into the WL analysis pipeline), the primary comparison
is with the linear result. The SSEE two-sector model thus brackets the KiDS
measurement: $S_8 \in [0.752, 0.820]$, encompassing the KiDS value $0.766 \pm 0.020$.

\paragraph{HMcode-2020 baryonic feedback correction.}
Applying the CLASS HMcode-2020 baryonic feedback suppression to the WDM baseline
($S_8^{\rm WDM} = 0.761$, CLASS), we obtain a baryonic suppression factor
$B_{\rm eff} = 0.9447$ (integrated over the CMB lensing $k$-range
$0.03\text{--}2.0\,h/\mathrm{Mpc}$ with the HMcode \texttt{hmcode\_version~=~2020\_baryonic\_feedback}
parameter), yielding:
\begin{equation}
  S_8^{\rm bar} = S_8^{\rm WDM}\times B_{\rm eff}^{1/2} = 0.761 \times 0.9721 = 0.758.
  \label{eq:S8_bar}
\end{equation}
This result is $0.06\sigma$ from DES~Y3 ($S_8 = 0.776\pm0.017$) and $0.40\sigma$ from
KiDS-1000 ($S_8 = 0.766\pm0.020$).

\textit{Calibration caveat.} HMcode-2020 \citep{Mead2021} was calibrated against
$\Lambda$CDM N-body simulations with $w=-1$ and $\sigma_8\approx0.8$.  Applied to
SSEE ($w_0=-0.840$, $w_a=-0.670$), the suppression factor $B_{\rm eff}$ carries an
estimated systematic uncertainty of $\lesssim5\%$, propagating to
$\Delta S_8^{\rm bar}\approx\pm0.02$.  The nominal result $S_8^{\rm bar}=0.758$
should therefore be interpreted as a central estimate within the range
$S_8^{\rm bar}\in[0.74,\,0.78]$ pending dedicated dark-energy N-body calibration.
The Level~2 step — running dedicated N-body simulations
(BAHAMAS, \citealt{McCarthy2017}; IllustrisTNG, \citealt{Nelson2019};
modified for SSEE dark energy) — requires $\sim$5000--20000 CPU-hours and is deferred
to a future HPC allocation.

\subsection{KiDS--DES internal tension and its interpretation in \SSEE}
\label{subsec:des}

The apparent discrepancy between our $S_8 = 0.820$ and the KiDS-1000 measurement
$S_8 = 0.766 \pm 0.020$ \citep{KiDS2021} and the DES Y3 measurement
$S_8 = 0.776 \pm 0.017$ \citep{DESY32022} requires careful decomposition.

\paragraph{Structure of the $S_8$ statistic.}
The quantity $S_8 \equiv \sigma_8\sqrt{\Omega_m/0.3}$ is constructed to be
approximately orthogonal to the weak-lensing amplitude degeneracy direction.
The normalisation $\Omega_m = 0.3$ is conventional and implicitly assumes the
inferred $\Omega_m$ is close to $0.3$.  In the \SSEE\ framework the relevant
matter density has two distinct values: $\Omega_{m,\mathrm{dyn}} = 0.160$
(dynamical dark matter sector) and $\Omega_{m,\mathrm{CMB}} = 0.3199$
(total including the $\phi$-DM clustering contribution active at
$k < k_{\rm fs}$).  Growth-rate observables probe $\Omega_{m,\mathrm{dyn}}$,
while the CMB acoustic geometry is sensitive to $\Omega_{m,\mathrm{CMB}}$.

\paragraph{Why KiDS and DES disagree internally.}
KiDS-1000 \citep{KiDS2021} and DES Y3 \citep{DESY32022} themselves differ by
$\Delta S_8 \approx 0.4\sigma$, which is sub-dominant but non-negligible.
This residual is driven by: (i) different angular scale cuts ($\ell_{\rm max}$);
(ii) differing baryonic feedback priors; and (iii) partially overlapping
shear calibration schemes.  These internal systematics shift $S_8$ at the
$\sim 1\%$ level — comparable to the statistical error — making a direct
sub-percent comparison to \SSEE\ premature.

\paragraph{The correct SSEE comparison.}
In the two-sector model the total matter clustering budget uses
$\Omega_m^{\rm tot} = \Omega_{\rm CDM} + \Omega_{\phi{\rm DM}} = 0.3199$
when $k < k_{\rm fs}$.  With this total, the \SSEE\ $S_8$ statistic evaluates
to $S_{8,\rm SSEE} = \sigma_8^{\rm eff} \sqrt{0.3199/0.3} = 0.820$, and
the tension with KiDS ($2.62\sigma$) and DES ($2.5\sigma$) are comparable.
Neither survey is singled out as anomalous with respect to \SSEE;
both sit $\sim 2.5\sigma$ below our prediction, consistent with the
unresolved $S_8$ tension that persists even in $\Lambda$CDM.

The improvement over single-sector \SSEE\ (Paper~5: $S_8 = 0.725$,
$1.96\sigma$ KiDS) is driven by the $\phi$-DM suppression of $\sigma_8^{\rm eff}$
from $0.702$ to $0.794$, which reduces the KiDS tension from positive to
the same sign as $\Lambda$CDM.  A joint DES+KiDS likelihood marginalised
over $(\sigma_8, \Omega_m)$ simultaneously — as will be provided by
Euclid Wide Survey weak lensing — will provide a definitive test.

\subsection{Compatibility with Lyman-$\alpha$ and WDM constraints}
\label{subsec:lya}

A potential objection is that $\mphi = 5.60\,\mathrm{eV}$ lies far below the
standard thermal-WDM Lyman-$\alpha$ bound $m_{\rm WDM} \gtrsim 3.3\text{--}3.5\,\mathrm{keV}$
\citep{Viel2005,Viel2013,PalanqueDelabrouille2020}.  We address this in three steps.

\paragraph{Step 1: $\phi$-DM is not thermal WDM.}
Thermal WDM bounds assume (a) 100\% of the dark matter is a single thermally
produced species, and (b) the momentum distribution is a Fermi-Dirac or
Bose-Einstein function at a single temperature.  Neither condition holds here.
The $\phi$-DM sector accounts for only half of the total matter budget
($\Omega_{\phiDM}/\Omega_{m}^{\rm tot} \simeq 0.50$).
The remaining sector is cold CDM ($\Omega_{\rm CDM} = 0.160$), which provides
full gravitational clustering at all scales $k < \kfs$ and is \emph{not}
suppressed.  For mixed dark matter models with a WDM fraction $f_{\rm WDM}$,
the Lyman-$\alpha$ constraint on the WDM component is substantially relaxed
(see e.g.\ \citealt{Boyarsky2019,Adhikari2017}); for $f_{\rm WDM} \approx 0.5$, the
effective bound weakens to $m_{\rm WDM}^{\rm eff} \gtrsim 0.1$--$0.5\,\mathrm{keV}$.

\paragraph{Step 2: The observable is $\kfs$, not $\mphi$ — and the production mechanism determines the mapping.}
The quantity constrained by Lyman-$\alpha$ forest measurements is the matter
power spectrum suppression wavenumber $\kfs$, not the particle mass directly.
For a given mass $\mphi$, the inferred $\kfs$ depends critically on the
production mechanism.

For a \emph{thermally} produced relic at $\mphi = 5.60\,\mathrm{eV}$
($= 5.60\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{keV}$), the \citet{Viel2005}
formula gives
\begin{equation}
  \kfs^{\rm thermal} = 23.7 \left(\frac{\mphi}{1\,\mathrm{keV}}\right)^{1.11}
  \!\left(\frac{\Omega_{\phiDM}h^2}{0.12}\right)^{0.15}
  \approx 0.070\;h/\mathrm{Mpc},
  \quad \lambda_{\rm fs}^{\rm thermal} \approx 90\;h^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}.
  \label{eq:kfs_thermal}
\end{equation}
At this scale, the $\phi$-DM sector would behave essentially as cold dark matter
on all observationally relevant scales ($k < 0.5\,h/\mathrm{Mpc}$), and the
$\sigma_8$ tension would not be resolved.

For a \emph{non-resonantly produced} (Dodelson-Widrow-like) component, the
free-streaming scale is set not by the thermal velocity but by the typical
production momentum; the approximate relation is \citep{Boyarsky2009}:
\begin{equation}
  \kfs^{\rm DW} = 7.68 \left(\frac{\mphi}{1\,\mathrm{keV}}\right)^{0.5}
  \!\left(\frac{\Omega_{\phiDM}h^2}{0.1}\right)^{0.5}
  \approx 0.487\;h/\mathrm{Mpc},
  \quad \lambda_{\rm fs}^{\rm DW} \approx 12.9\;h^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}.
  \label{eq:kfs_DW}
\end{equation}
The ratio $\kfs^{\rm DW}/\kfs^{\rm thermal}\approx 7.0$ reflects the fact that
non-resonant production gives a colder phase-space distribution per unit mass
than thermal equilibrium.
The SSEE two-sector value $\kfs = 0.493\,h/\mathrm{Mpc}$ is consistent with
the DW estimate (Eq.~\ref{eq:kfs_DW}) to within $1.2\%$, well within the
calibration uncertainty of both formulas.

\textbf{Key point:} the choice of production mechanism is not arbitrary.
The algebraic mass $\mphi = 5.60\,\mathrm{eV}$ (Section~\ref{sec:mass}),
combined with the thermal formula, gives $\kfs^{\rm thermal}\approx 0.070$
— too small to suppress $\sigma_8$ at $k_\sigma\approx 0.125\,h/\mathrm{Mpc}$.
The same mass with the DW formula gives $\kfs^{\rm DW}\approx 0.487$, which
lies exactly in the window required to resolve the $f\sigma_8$ tension.
This non-trivial coincidence constitutes the primary predictive claim of the
two-sector construction: a non-thermal production mechanism is required by
the algebraic mass, and the required mechanism predicts a Lyman-$\alpha$
signature within current observational reach.

\textbf{Validity caveat.}  The DW formula was calibrated for non-resonantly
produced sterile neutrinos at $m_s\sim\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{keV})$; applying it
at $\mphi = 5.60\,\mathrm{eV}$ constitutes a three-order-of-magnitude
extrapolation in mass.  In the absence of a full Boltzmann hierarchy for the
$\phi$-DM phase-space distribution, $\kfs = 0.493\,h/\mathrm{Mpc}$ should
be treated as an \emph{order-of-magnitude estimate} to be refined by future
IS-EFT Boltzmann calculations.
A direct Lyman-$\alpha$ test should be formulated in terms of the predicted
suppression scale $\kfs$, not the thermal-WDM mass bound.

\paragraph{Step 3: What remains observable.}
At scales $k > \kfs = 0.493\,h/\mathrm{Mpc}$ (relevant for the Lyman-$\alpha$
forest at $z \sim 2$--$4$), the CDM sector still contributes
$(\Omega_{\rm CDM}/\Omega_m^{\rm tot}) \approx 50\%$ of the power.  Expanding
the mixing formula $P_{\rm mix}/P_{\rm \Lambda CDM} = [(1-\fphi) + \fphi\,T_{\rm WDM}]^2$
gives
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\Delta P(k)}{P_{\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}}} =
  -2\fphi(1-\fphi)(1-T_{\rm WDM})
  - \fphi^2\,(1-T_{\rm WDM})^2,
  \label{eq:power_suppression}
\end{equation}
with $\fphi \equiv f_{\phiDM} \approx 0.5$.  In two asymptotic regimes:
\begin{alignat}{2}
  k \ll \kfs:& \quad T_{\rm WDM}\to 1,\quad
    &\frac{\Delta P}{P} &\to 0, \\
  k \gg \kfs:& \quad T_{\rm WDM}\to 0,\quad
    &\frac{\Delta P}{P} &\to -\fphi(2-\fphi) \simeq -0.75.
    \label{eq:suppression_asymp}
\end{alignat}
At $k = \kfs$ the CLASS-calibrated value $T_{\rm WDM}(\kfs)\approx 0.19$
gives $\Delta P/P \approx -0.65$, i.e.\ $65\%$ suppression.
The dominant contribution is the linear term $-2\fphi(1-\fphi)(1-T_{\rm WDM})$;
the quadratic $-\fphi^2(1-T_{\rm WDM})^2$ adds only $\approx 10\%$ of that.
A dedicated Lyman-$\alpha$ likelihood analysis
(planned with the IS-EFT framework) is required to verify that this
suppression profile is consistent with observed forest data.  Such analysis
constitutes a \emph{falsification test}: if the forest shows no suppression
at $k \approx 0.5\,h/\mathrm{Mpc}$, the two-sector model is excluded.

The observational predictions of this paper — the proposed $\fsig$ resolution and
$\sigeff = 0.794$ — depend only on the two-sector growth factor
$G_{\rm 2s} = 0.979$ at scales $k < \kfs$, and are robust to the
Lyman-$\alpha$ issue above.

\subsection{The $H(z)$ tension}
\label{subsec:Hz}

Paper~5 reported $H(z)$ tension ($\chi^2_r = 1.861$ on cosmic chronometers) as a
structural consequence of $\Omega_{\rm m,dyn} = 0.160$: the background expansion is
driven by the dynamical sector alone, giving a faster expansion at intermediate
redshifts. The two-sector extension does not alter the background Friedmann equation
and therefore does not affect $H(z)$. This tension is the remaining target for
future work.

\subsection{Physical origin of the mass relation}
\label{subsec:origin}

The relation $\mphi = \mnu \times \Halg$ connects two quantities derived independently
in Paper~4 via the acoustic residual operator. We note:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\mnu = 0.0824\,\mathrm{eV}$ is the sum of active neutrino masses
    \citep{Lesgourgues2006} derived from the IS thermal residual. It arises from the coupling of the IS sector to the
    baryon-photon plasma.
  \item $\Halg = 67.96\,\mathrm{km/s/Mpc}$ is the expansion rate set by the IS
    relaxation horizon.
  \item The product $\mnu \times \Halg$ in natural units has dimensions
    $[\mathrm{eV}]\times[\mathrm{km/s/Mpc}]$. The numerical value 5.60 in
    electron-volts arises when $\Halg$ is taken as a dimensionless number — i.e., as
    $H_0$ in units of $\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$.
\end{enumerate}

The physical interpretation is: the $\phi$-DM field acquires a characteristic
energy scale set by the same IS relaxation operator that governs active neutrino
production. This is structurally analogous to the seesaw mechanism \citep{Minkowski1977},
where a sterile-sector mass is related to the active neutrino mass through a common
operator --- but, as established in Section~\ref{subsec:production}, the analogy
is dimensional only: $\phi$-DM is \emph{not} a sterile neutrino.
The field content is fixed by the action~\eqref{eq:phiDM_lagrangian}: $\phiDM$
is a real scalar boson, populated by gravitational particle production rather
than by active--sterile mixing.  The remaining open item is the \emph{ab initio}
relic abundance integral, not the field's identity.

\subsection{Status of the zero-parameter claim}
\label{subsec:zero_params}

The \SSEE\ philosophy requires that no number be \emph{fitted} to data. We verify:

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Omega_{\phiDM} = (\MIRA - 1)\,\Omega_{\rm m,dyn}$: derived from the
    MIRA algebraic identity. Not fitted.
  \item $\mphi = \mnu \times \Halg$: both factors derive from Paper~4 algebraic
    operators. Not fitted.
  \item $G_{\rm 2s} = 0.979$: computed by integrating the growth ODE with
    $\Omega_{\rm m,growth} = 0.320$ (fixed by the two sectors above). Not fitted.
  \item $\sigeff = 0.794$: derived from $G_{\rm 2s}$ and the Planck reference
    $\sigma_{8,\Lambda{\rm CDM}} = 0.811$. Not fitted.
\end{itemize}

The two-sector extension adds zero free parameters to the model. The remaining
question is the derivation of the physical coupling and the free-streaming scale
$\kfs$ from first principles within the IS-EFT framework, which we leave for
future work.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We have proposed a phenomenological two-sector resolution for the $f\sigma_8$ growth-rate tension identified in Paper~5 of the
\SSEE\ series. The key results are:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{EFT scan}: kinetic braiding ($\alpha_B$) is a no-go — it suppresses
    growth rather than enhancing it. Run-rate modification ($\alpha_M = -0.08$)
    reduces the tension to $1.57\sigma$ but requires tuning to the observational
    bound.

  \item \textbf{Two-sector model}: the MIRA algebraic identity
    $\MIRA \approx 2$ implies a second dark matter sector,
    $\Omega_{\phiDM} = (\MIRA-1)\,\Omega_{\rm m,dyn} = 0.160$, with a
    free-streaming cutoff at $\kfs$.

  \item \textbf{Algebraic mass}: $\mphi = \mnu^{\rm active} \times \Halg =
    0.0824 \times 67.96 = 5.60\,\mathrm{eV}$, derived from the acoustic residual
    operator and the algebraic Hubble constant of Paper~4. Deviation from the
    Dodelson-Widrow target ($5.74\,\mathrm{eV}$): $2.4\%$. Zero new free parameters.

  \item \textbf{Verification}: two-sector growth factor $G_{\rm 2s} = 0.979$
    gives $\sigeff = 0.794$ ($S_8 = 0.820$, $2.62\sigma$ KiDS, better than
    $\Lambda$CDM $3.27\sigma$) and $f\sigma_8$ mean tension $\mathbf{0.50\sigma}$
    (from $2.56\sigma$ for the single-sector \SSEE\ baseline, $\sigma_{8,\rm base}=0.702$; comparable to $\Lambda$CDM $0.51\sigma$).
    All background tests — CMB ($\Delta\mathrm{BIC} = -31.3$), BAO ($\chi^2_r = 0.01$),
    clusters ($\chi^2_r = 0.122$) — conserved.
\end{enumerate}

The framework \SSEE-V3.6 now offers a unified linear-level description across six categories of observational tests
(background expansion, CMB, BAO, galaxy clusters, weak lensing, and RSD growth
rate). The growth tension is reduced phenomenologically
via the two-sector growth factor alone, without invoking any warm dark matter
transfer function. However, as explicitly discussed, the mass coupling $m_\phi = \Sigma m_\nu H_0^{\rm alg}$ remains a numerological ansatz pending derivation from a complete lagrangian, and the free-streaming scale $k_{\rm fs}$ requires verification against Lyman-$\alpha$ forest data. The remaining background tension ($H(z)$, $\chi^2_r = 1.861$)
is structural and traceable to $\Omega_{\rm m,dyn} = 0.160$.

The \textbf{falsifiable predictions} of this paper are: (1) the $\phi$-dark
matter free-streaming scale $\kfs = 0.493\,h/$Mpc, set by the algebraic mass
$\mphi = 5.60\,\mathrm{eV}$ and probed by the Lyman-$\alpha$ forest and by
DESI~Y3 / Euclid galaxy clustering (2026--2028) --- the relevant channel
because $\phiDM$ is a gravitationally produced scalar with no Standard-Model
portal (Section~\ref{subsec:production}), and so leaves no neutrino-sector
signature; and (2) $\sigeff = 0.794$ ($S_8 = 0.820$), testable by Euclid
weak-lensing (2026--2028).
